**The two cards are not equivalent on heavy prefill cells.** Card 21 holds
2325–2400 MHz in compute-heavy cells, while card 22 and C1, the card of the
earlier tables, raise the SW power-cap flag in the same cells and drop to
2085–2272 MHz. A cross-check that ran each family's fso rows on the other card
confirms that this is the card and not the family: the Family B layer at
M = 8192 reads 1694 µs on card 21, 1796 µs on card 22 and 1773 µs on C1. The
ratios inside each table are like-for-like, because fso and its comparators
share a card. The absolute prefill numbers of Family A and Family B (card 21)
are not comparable with Family C's (card 22) or with the earlier C1 tables.

**The three MoE rows, 2026-09-28 (warm-protocol history; the deltas below were
measured before the cold-weight protocol and describe the code changes, not
the published numbers).** Two changes moved them against the
2026-09-04 run: the fused-SwiGLU FC1 epilogue, which did not exist for sm_120
then, and the multi-CTA routing builder, which was enabled for sm_120 on this
date. Layer µs fell on 13 of 14 cells in each table, median −1.2 % to −1.6 %,
reaching −7 % to −9 % at M ≥ 1024 where the routing builder engages (it needs
`M * topk >= 4096`). The per-projection grouped GEMM tables in
[`../gemm/sm120.md`](../gemm/sm120.md) came from the same run and moved by a
median +0.05 % to +0.18 %, which is the control: neither change touches a bare
GEMM, so those tables say the run is comparable with the old one.

Two cells did not improve, and both are measurement resolution rather than
code — do not read them as regressions:

* **Family B M = 1 read 24.7 µs against 22.6 µs (warm).** The whole M = 1 band
  quantises to ~2.05 µs steps under graph replay (24 measurements of a width
  sweep at M = 1 landed on six distinct values spaced 2.05 µs apart), so this
  cell cannot resolve better than one tick, which is 9 % of it. The fused FC1
  lands one tick up here and one tick *down* at other widths.
* **Family C M = 4 read 50.0 µs in the torch 2.11 run of this date against
  41.1 µs on 2026-09-04, and read 41.4 µs in the torch 2.13 rerun of that
  date.** That cell's 30 active experts hold 94 MB of weights against a 96 MB
  L2, so its time depends on which side of the L2 a routing draw falls on:
  re-drawing the routing with six seeds moves the fused-vs-unfused verdict from
  −9.4 % to +21.4 % and the absolute time from 39 to 59 µs. The two same-day
  runs landing 9 µs apart on the same draw is that bimodality, not a change.

Consequently the ±1 % acceptance rule in [`../README.md`](../README.md) cannot
be applied to the M ≤ 4 rows of these tables, nor to any cell whose active
weight footprint lands within a few MB of the 96 MB L2 (Family C M = 4–5,
Family B M = 3–4). Judge those by whether they moved more than one tick, and
average over routing draws before calling one of them a regression.

**Cold weights, 2026-09-28 (the protocol of every MoE row and column in this
file).** Replaying one cell's graph with the same weights had served the
M ≤ 2 (Family B) and M ≤ 4 (Family C) cells from the 96 MB L2 — the active
expert set of those cells fits it — while a serving step streams every layer's
weights through the card between two uses of the same layer. The captured
graph now holds R copies of the cell over R copies of the weights (same
values, distinct memory, every quantized form built per copy), with R × active
weight bytes ≥ 2 × L2 (R = 2–256, 2–8 on these layer cells; a 1–8 × L2 sweep converged at 2 ×,
`sm120_coldl2_20260928/l2mult`), and the reported µs is the replay time / R;
activations, routing and the intermediate slabs stay warm, as the previous
kernel just produced them in a model, and for the kernel-level cells they are
the same tensors for every copy (giving each copy its own activation buffers
had cost the M = 8192 `gate_up` cell 8 %, an artefact). What it moved, warm →
cold: Family B M = 1 22.7 → 35.1 µs, M = 2 30.9 → 61.4, M ≥ 4 unchanged;
Family C M = 1 18.5 → 26.9, M = 2 22.8 → 43.9, M = 4 39.6 → 73.5, M ≥ 8
unchanged (measured on the v6 build; the prologue round below then took the
M = 1 cells to the 34.5 / 25.8 published); every comparator moved the same
way (vLLM FP8 M = 1 22.8 → 38.6).
The single-copy protocol had also charged one `cudaGraphLaunch` gap (~2 µs on
this card) to every cell; the rotation amortises it over R copies, which is
why some dense cells below 20 µs read lower now than warm. Rows carry
`weight_copies`.

**Decode gate v6, 2026-09-28.**
The grouped MXFP8 cascade's decode gate chose the solo (16,128,4) instance at
one CTA per SM whenever `m_cap <= 4`; since the row cap became
`round_up(M, 4)` that covered M = 1, 2 and 4, while the sweep that wrote the
gate had wanted only M = 1 on it. The gate now reads the op's
`max_active_groups` bound and takes the solo instance only when its
worst-case tile count is one full wave (0.6–1 × the SM count); otherwise the
2-CTA/SM (16,64,4). Decided on the routed layer under the warm protocol of that morning, two
interleaved passes on each of two cards: Family B M = 1 −8.2 %, M = 2 −5.3 %,
Family C M = 1 −9.9 %, M = 4 −4.2 %, nothing at M ≥ 8. Re-validated under the
cold-weight protocol (`sm120_coldl2_20260928/oldgate_*`, old gate via
`FSO_MOE_DECODE_GATE=0` against the published rows): Family B M = 1 +0.3 %,
M = 2 −2.6 %, M = 4 −0.4 %; Family C M = 1 −1.2 %, M = 2 −0.1 %, M = 4 −0.6 %;
M = 8 ±0.1 % — the warm wins were mostly an L2 artefact (the FC2 launched
under PDL packed its CTAs better when the weights came from L2), and with the
weights streamed from DRAM the two routes sit under the same bandwidth. The
gate stays: it never loses, and Family B M = 2 keeps −2.6 %. The isolated
`gate_up` kernel cells in [`../gemm/sm120.md`](../gemm/sm120.md) are on the
gate's 2-CTA instance at M = 1–4; under cold weights that costs +0.9 % at
Family B M = 1 and +6 % at Family C M = 1 against the solo instance. The layer
cell decides, by [`../README.md`](../README.md) §8.

**Prologue round, 2026-09-28 (the build behind every fso row in this file
from this date).** With the weights cold, the grouped GEMM's own launch
overhead is what separates the decode-band cells from the DRAM floor, so the
round instrumented the kernel's prologue (`globaltimer` stamps behind a
build-time `-DFSO_PROLOGUE_TRACE`, `../../api/compat.md`) rather than its tile
shapes. A launch with no work had cost 2.2 µs against the Triton kernel's 0.8:
0.66 µs of `masked_m` L2 latency, 0.50 µs (E = 128) / 0.92 µs (E = 256) of one
warp scanning the per-group row counts serially, the rest barriers and PDL
release; the launch shape itself was not the cause (an empty kernel of the
same shape costs the same 0.5–0.6 µs on both sides). Two changes landed: the
prefix scan over the groups runs block-wide (12 warps, a 12-entry smem total
table), which takes the empty launch to 1.6 µs at either E; and the grid is
capped at the tile upper bound `max_active_groups × ceil(m_cap / TileM) ×
ceil(N / TileN)` instead of two CTAs per SM, so at M = 1 the FC1 launches 192
CTAs instead of 340 and the FC2 128 (Family B) — under PDL the 340 CTAs had
entered over a 2.9 µs window, because the ones landing on SMs still held by
the previous kernel wait, and the capped grid enters within 0.1 µs. Bit-exact
against the previous build, dense kernels' SASS unchanged. Accepted on the
routed layer under the cold protocol on `C1`, two interleaved passes per arm
(`sm120_coldl2_20260928/prologue_arm`, pass spread ≤ 0.2 %): Family B M = 1
35.1 → 34.5 µs (−1.9 %), M = 2 −0.6 %, M = 4–128 within +0.3 %; Family C
M = 1 26.9 → 25.9 (−3.7 %), M = 2 −1.2 %, M = 4 −0.9 %, M = 8–128 −0.2 to
−0.3 %; the isolated kernels: Family C `gate_up` M = 1 14.8 → 13.7 (−7.5 %),
`down` M = 1 −2.8 %, Family B `down` M = 1 −1.6 %. The +0.2–0.3 % on
Family B M = 4–16 is a register side effect, not the scan: nvcc 13.0 now
allocates 69 registers instead of 64 to the FC2's (16,128,2) instance at two
CTAs per SM, and the fused combine kernel that used to co-reside next to two
such CTAs no longer fits, so it launches a little later; making the combine
128 threads or ≤ 40 registers removes the dependence on the FC2 landing on
exactly 64. What is left in the prologue: the FC1's prologue is always
exposed (its CTAs enter only 0.1–0.5 µs before the gather-quantize ends);
the round cut the exposed part from 1.28 → 0.51 µs (Family B M = 1) and 1.66
→ 0.51 (Family C), and the remaining 0.5–0.9 µs needs the first tile's
weight TMA (or the tile itself) issued before the PDL wait, or the
gather-quantize folded into routing so the FC1 launches during routing's
tail.

**Round 2, 2026-09-29 (the build behind every fso row in this file from this
date): the combine kernel and the FC2's partial tiles.** Two changes, each a
fixed cost that the cold protocol had left standing next to the DRAM floor.
(1) The combine. The layer's last kernel, the weighted combine, launched
256-thread blocks; under PDL it starts early only if one of its CTAs fits on an
SM beside the FC2's resident CTAs, and since the prologue round the Family B
decode FC2 — the (16,128,2) instance at two CTAs per SM — used 69 registers
(72 allocated), so two of its CTAs left 10,240 registers of the SM's 65,536,
enough for a 128-thread combine CTA at 64 registers (8,192) but not for a
256-thread one (16,384). On sm_120 the combine now launches 128-thread blocks
while its grid is at most 256 such blocks (M ≤ 128 at HIDDEN = 2048); the
combine's first CTA is resident within about 1 µs of the FC2's release again
(CUPTI: it had started 21–116 µs after the release at Family B M = 4–128), and
the kernel by itself is 0.2–0.3 µs faster on small grids. The kernel also
issues its routing-input loads (`slot_of_flat`, `topk_w`: routing outputs, two
or more launches upstream, so grandparent-or-older data like every pre-wait
read in the chain) before its PDL wait, which takes one dependent round trip
off the post-release path — 0.26 µs where that round trip was an L2 hit
(M ≤ 2) and 0.44–0.54 µs where it went to DRAM (M ≥ 4). A register cap on the
256-thread form (≤ 40 registers) was tried and spills, so it was not taken.
Measured on `C1`, cold, two interleaved passes per arm, pass spread ≤ 0.2 %
(`sm120_r2_p1_20260929`): the launch change alone Family B −0.1 to −0.8 % over
the decode band and Family C −0.0 to −1.0 %, the hoist on top of it a further
−0.1 to −0.9 % on all 16 decode cells; prefill within ±0.2 %; bit-identical
outputs; on the H200 the hoist (the only part live there) is −0.1 to −0.7 %
with the nine sm_90 test files passing. (2) The FC2's partial tiles. Each
expert owns `m_cap` rows of the output slab, so the last row block of every
expert is partly padding — at decode one or two routed rows of sixteen — and
the FC2's plain epilogue wrote every tile's whole TileM × TileN box with a TMA
store, padding rows included; per-tile traces (a stamped copy of the kernel)
showed that store waiting 2.1–2.5 µs per tile in the SM's TMA queue behind the
loads. A partial tile's valid rows are now written directly by the store warp
as 16-byte stores and full tiles keep the TMA store; the valid rows are
bit-identical, the tile and the accumulation order are unchanged, the dense
instances' SASS is unchanged. Measured the same way (`sm120_r2_p3_20260929`):
the Family C `down` kernel cells M = 4–4096 all improve (median −2.1 %,
M = 4096 −7.4 %, M = 8192 −6.4 %; the cell now beats vLLM's Triton kernel at
M = 4096), the Family B `down` control −2.5 to −6.2 % from M = 16, the routed
layers up to −1.5 % (Family C) / −1.1 % (Family B) at M = 16–128 and up to
−2.9 % / −1.7 % in prefill, worst cell +0.4 % at M = 512 in both families. The
same round measured, and rejected with numbers: a two-stage scale-factor ring
(the single SF stage's exposed wait is at most 1.1–3.1 µs per CTA and only at
decode; the prototype gave +0.1 % / 0.0 %), computing the next tile's scheduler
step during the ring waits (−0.04 %), a tile change for the Family C `down`
(the 1-CTA (16,64,4) is 5.4 % faster on the isolated kernel cell at M = 1024
and 5.4 % slower on the layer — the layer decides, `../README.md` §8), and the
shared-D-buffer hypothesis (no grouped instance aliases D with the A/B
stages). After the change Family C `down` still trails vLLM's kernel by
2.4 / 6.0 / 13.7 µs at M = 16 / 64 / 1024: from M = 1024 up every tile waits
for its first stage of data, and MXFP8's scale bytes are 3.1 % more traffic
than the block scales the Triton kernel reads.
(3) The grouped GEMM's pre-wait window. After the prologue round the gap from
a GEMM's PDL release to its first tile was 0.9–1.3 µs on every GEMM of the
layer — 0.3–0.4 µs of release latency plus 0.5–0.65 µs of the first
`get_next_block()` in each warp role — and the first stage's cold weight fill
followed it. Both the first scheduler step and the weight half of the first
stage's loads depend only on kernel parameters and grandparent-or-older data
(the shared-memory prefix built from `masked_m`, which the routing kernel wrote
two launches upstream; the expert weights and their scale factors, which no
kernel of the chain writes), so every warp role now constructs its scheduler
and takes its first tile before `cudaGridDependencySynchronize()`, and the
A/B-load and SF-load warps arm the first stage's barriers for both halves and
issue the B and SFB copies before the wait, the A and SFA copies after it; the
trigger stays right after the wait. A traced layer shows the FC2's first tile
starting at its release (0.38–0.42 µs after the FC1 ends instead of 0.90–1.06)
and its first scale stage landing 0.7–1.1 µs after the FC1 instead of 2.9–6.3,
which takes 1.4–4.0 µs off the FC2's critical-path increment at M ≤ 4; the
FC1's own first tile does not move (its wait is already satisfied when its
CTAs reach it) and its increment grows 0.1–2.3 µs because the FC2's early
weight copies share DRAM with its stream — the span still falls at every cell.
Pre-issuing all stages instead of one loses at M = 1 (the extra copies slow
the short, DRAM-bound predecessor about as much as they save the successor)
and wins at M ≥ 2, so one stage is what ships. The change is confined to the
grouped instantiations, because applied to the shared kernel code it made the
narrow-N dense GEMMs at M ≤ 64 4–14 % slower: a dense launch keeps two
persistent CTAs per SM of which only 40 of 170 hold a tile at M = 4, and with
the first tile scheduled before the wait the idle CTAs leave their SMs
sooner relative to the successor's launch, which moves the successor's tile
CTAs onto a different fixed set of SMs where the k-loop runs 40 % longer (a
placement effect, reproduced 100 of 100 samples; the grouped launches are
capped at their tile bound, so they have no idle CTAs and are immune); the
wide dense shapes gained 1–8 % from the same change, which a dense grid cap
at the tile count could recover in a later round. Measured on `C2`, cold, two
interleaved passes per arm against the build of (1)–(2) (`sm120_r2_p2_20260929`):
Family B layer M = 1 33.8 → 32.5 µs (−4.0 %), M = 2 −2.6 %, M = 4 −3.5 %,
M = 8–128 −0.5 to −1.3 %; Family C M = 1 25.2 → 23.9 (−5.1 %), M = 2 −4.8 %,
M = 4 −3.2 %, M = 8–128 −0.3 to −1.5 %; the isolated `down` kernel −6.1 % /
−19.3 % at M = 1 (Family B / C); every one of the 48 decode cells faster,
prefill within +0.2 %, dense kernels byte-identical, outputs bit-identical
under eleven knob settings. Accepted on `C1` against the build of (1)–(2), two
interleaved passes (`sm120_r2_accept2_20260929`, pass spread ≤ 0.3 %): all 48
decode cells faster (Family B layer M = 1 33.9 → 32.5 µs, M = 2 −2.7 %, M = 4
−3.4 %; Family C M = 1 25.2 → 24.0, M = 2 −4.8 %, M = 4 −3.0 %; the isolated
`down` kernel M = 1 −6.0 % / −20.0 %), the routed + shared block −3.2 % /
−3.7 % at M = 1 / 2, prefill layer cells within +0.1 %, the thirteen test files
passing, and the dense instances' SASS identical function by function. One
kernel-level prefill cell moved: Family C `down` at M = 2048 reads +1.2 % on two
passes, with the new arm bimodal (228 and 237 µs) where the old arm is not —
in that cell the graph's next copy of the same GEMM pre-issues its weight
loads under the previous copy's tail, which is a bench artefact of chaining a
GEMM behind itself; the layer cell at M = 2048, whose successor is the combine,
is flat.

## Grouped GEMM kernel-level comparison — the Triton grouped GEMM inside `fused_experts`

The layer tables above compare whole layers, glue included. This section
isolates one projection: fso's grouped MXFP8 GEMM cell — the same
`moe.gate_up` / `moe.down` rows as [`../gemm/sm120.md`](../gemm/sm120.md),
masked-slab layout with `m_cap` rows per expert — against the Triton
block-scaled grouped GEMM that sglang's and vLLM's `fused_experts` run for that
projection. The comparator cell is that one kernel over
`moe_align_block_size`-sorted rows with the routing weight not applied, on the
same routing draw as the fso cell, so both sides compute exactly the same
expert rows; the activation quantize sits outside the timed region on both
sides (fso: MXFP8 1×32 UE8M0; Triton FP8: per-token-group-128 activations
against 128×128 weight blocks, fp32 scales; Triton BF16: none). Neither library
ships a tuned tile json for this device at these shapes, so both run their
`get_default_config` tile — the versions and this caveat are in the comparator
table under Provenance. Every cell is a graph-replay median on the same card
as its family's fso rows (card 21 for Family B, card 22 for Family C) at the
same clock lock, and cos is against the same bf16 reference for every
column.
