## Reading notes

Because of the ~2 µs graph-replay tick and the routing-draw sensitivity of
cells near the L2 size (`../README.md` §8), the ±1 % acceptance rule in
[`../README.md`](../README.md) cannot
be applied to the M ≤ 4 rows of these tables, nor to any cell whose active
weight footprint lands within a few MB of the 96 MB L2 (Family C M = 4–5,
Family B M = 3–4). Judge those by whether they moved more than one tick, and
average over routing draws before calling one of them a regression.

**Cold weights, 2026-09-28 (the protocol of every MoE row and column in this
file).** Replaying one cell's graph with the same weights had served the
M ≤ 2 (Family B) and M ≤ 4 (Family C) cells from the 96 MB L2 — the active
expert set of those cells fits it — while a serving step streams every layer's
weights through the card between two uses of the same layer. The captured
graph now holds R copies of the cell over R copies of the weights (same
values, distinct memory, every quantized form built per copy), with R × active
weight bytes ≥ 2 × L2 (R = 2–256, 2–8 on these layer cells; a 1–8 × L2 sweep converged at 2 ×,
`sm120_coldl2_20260928/l2mult`), and the reported µs is the replay time / R;
activations, routing and the intermediate slabs stay warm, as the previous
kernel just produced them in a model, and for the kernel-level cells they are
the same tensors for every copy (giving each copy its own activation buffers
had cost the M = 8192 `gate_up` cell 8 %, an artefact). What it moved, warm →
cold: Family B M = 1 22.7 → 35.1 µs, M = 2 30.9 → 61.4, M ≥ 4 unchanged;
Family C M = 1 18.5 → 26.9, M = 2 22.8 → 43.9, M = 4 39.6 → 73.5, M ≥ 8
unchanged (measured on the v6 build; the prologue round (History) then took the
M = 1 cells to the 34.5 / 25.8 published then); every comparator moved the same
way (vLLM FP8 M = 1 22.8 → 38.6).
The single-copy protocol had also charged one `cudaGraphLaunch` gap (~2 µs on
this card) to every cell; the rotation amortises it over R copies, which is
why some dense cells below 20 µs read lower now than warm. Rows carry
`weight_copies`.

## Family A — Qwen3-4B dense MLP block

`gate_up` (N=19456, K=2560) → silu·mul → `down` (N=2560, K=9728), activation
quantize inside the graph. BSFP8 uses the fused `quantize_1x128_fp8_packed`
and a `torch.compile`d silu·mul; MXFP8 uses the fused
`silu_chunk_mul_quantize_1x32_fp8` (no BF16 intermediate). `model ms` =
µs × 36 layers / 1000.

The serving-library columns run the same block with the BSFP8 column's block-FP8
recipe (1×128 activation scales, 128×128 weight scales): sglang's block-FP8
linear and vLLM's, described under Comparators (cuBLAS's block-FP8 `scaled_mm`
is n/a on sm_120). They are read from
`tests/baselines/ref_mlp_qwen3_4b_5090.jsonl`, measured in a separate run on the
same card under the same clock lock 1.2 hours after the fso rows. Each column
title names the GEMM backend its cells recorded, and each comparator has a ×fso
BSFP8 column: its µs divided by the BSFP8 µs of the same row, so a value above 1
means fso is ahead. On this card `fso.dense` serves a block-FP8 checkpoint
through the MXFP8 column's path, requantizing the weights to MXFP8 1×32 at load
([`../../api/dense.md`](../../api/dense.md)); the reading below sets both
comparators against both fso columns.

tiles: since the prologue round of 2026-09-28 an empty launch costs fso 1.6 µs

CTAs instead of 340 and the FC2 128 (Family B) — under PDL the 340 CTAs had
entered over a 2.9 µs window, because the ones landing on SMs still held by
the previous kernel wait, and the capped grid enters within 0.1 µs. Bit-exact
against the previous build, dense kernels' SASS unchanged. Accepted on the
routed layer under the cold protocol on `C1`, two interleaved passes per arm
(`sm120_coldl2_20260928/prologue_arm`, pass spread ≤ 0.2 %): Family B M = 1

date): the combine kernel and the FC2's partial tiles.** Two changes, each a
fixed cost that the cold protocol had left standing next to the DRAM floor.
(1) The combine. The layer's last kernel, the weighted combine, launched
256-thread blocks; under PDL it starts early only if one of its CTAs fits on an
SM beside the FC2's resident CTAs, and since the prologue round the Family B
decode FC2 — the (16,128,2) instance at two CTAs per SM — used 69 registers
(72 allocated), so two of its CTAs left 10,240 registers of the SM's 65,536,
enough for a 128-thread combine CTA at 64 registers (8,192) but not for a
256-thread one (16,384). On sm_120 the combine now launches 128-thread blocks
while its grid is at most 256 such blocks (M ≤ 128 at HIDDEN = 2048); the
combine's first CTA is resident within about 1 µs of the FC2's release again
(CUPTI: it had started 21–116 µs after the release at Family B M = 4–128), and
the kernel by itself is 0.2–0.3 µs faster on small grids. The kernel also
issues its routing-input loads (`slot_of_flat`, `topk_w`: routing outputs, two
or more launches upstream, so grandparent-or-older data like every pre-wait
read in the chain) before its PDL wait, which takes one dependent round trip
off the post-release path — 0.26 µs where that round trip was an L2 hit
(M ≤ 2) and 0.44–0.54 µs where it went to DRAM (M ≥ 4). A register cap on the
256-thread form (≤ 40 registers) was tried and spills, so it was not taken.
