All runs: the B300 SXM6 AC at PCI `E6:00.0` of pod `fish-dev-5f7c8f8bbf-hlvt4`,
**clocks unlocked** (no lock is available on this pod), every cell preceded by
`FSO_BENCH_WARM_MS=300` and taken under a `flock` that keeps one process on the
device. Driver 595.84, torch 2.11.0+cu130, CUDA 13.0, CUTLASS 4.4.2 for the
ahead-of-time kernels and `nvidia-cutlass-dsl` **4.8.0** in the virtual
environment for the CuTe-DSL rows, built `ARCH=10.0f`; commit `f790fe3` plus
the uncommitted MXFP8 change set, squashed as **this commit**. The dense decode
rows of Family A require the `sm100` extra (`nvidia-cutlass-dsl` 4.5.0 or
newer) and are **inert on 4.4.2**: below that floor the router behaves exactly
as it did before the row existed, so a deployment pinned to 4.4.2 reads the
dense decode numbers of the tiers behind the row rather than these. The full
environment table, the mechanisms this run added (in the MoE layer the slot
route's row-capacity clause is now per kernel — the plain slot kernel runs to
its full 64-wide token tile, the fused-SwiGLU slot kernel to one 32-column
epilogue chunk — and the pointer-array route reads its per-group problem
shapes from the routing kernel instead of running an argument-preparation
kernel ahead of each grouped GEMM; on the dense path the CuTe-DSL decode row
in front of the three tiers now reaches M ≤ 64 with a 64-wide token tile on
the 2-CTA 256-row weight tile), the unlocked-device caveats
(about ±3 % at M ≥ 4096, the wider scatter of the M = 8192 MLP-block cells,
clock droop at M = 8192, the ~2.05 µs replay tick) and the L2 caveat are in
[`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md); they apply to every row here.

Every comparator row on this page was measured on the same card, in the same
session and on the same tree as the fso rows it sits next to, with the same
warm-up and the same graph-replay protocol. Family A's two cuBLAS columns are
cells of the same bench pass as its fso columns and share that pass's inputs.
The MoE comparators are separate passes, run at the end of the same session
under the same GPU lock, driven by the same routing generator and seed as the
fso passes, so both sides see the same expert assignment at every M. To bound
how much those comparator columns move on their own, all three torch files were
measured a second time in the same session: over the eighty-four cells, the
median pass-to-pass difference is under half a per cent.

The tables on this page have been measured on three different cards of the
same pod — `86:00.0` on 2026-09-15, `06:00.0` on 2026-09-17 and `E6:00.0` for
the 2026-09-22 runs — and each card change forced a wholesale replacement
rather than a merge, because rows from two cards are not comparable under this
protocol. How much the card alone was worth was measured when it changed: the
comparator columns of this page, which contain no fso kernel and cannot move
with a library change, read about 1.8 % faster on `E6:00.0` than the same
columns of the 2026-09-17 run. This run is the second one on `E6:00.0`, so it
replaces its predecessor for a code reason rather than a card reason, and the
comparator columns are the check on that: they are reported next to the
published ones in the run's `tables/TABLES_comparators.txt`.

Two structural notes on the MoE comparators. First, neither sglang nor
deep_gemm is installed on this pod, so the comparator columns here are not the
ones `sm90.md` and `sm120.md` carry; a column of this file is not comparable to
a column of those. Second, the torch rows and the fso rows share the routing
generator and its seed, so they see the same expert assignment, but the torch
layer computes exactly `M · 8` rows of GEMM while the masked-slab layout
computes `E · m_cap` rows and discards the padding — the two are comparable as
time per layer, not as arithmetic done per layer. The routing itself is uniform
(`randperm(E)[:8]` per token), which is easier for every grouped implementation
than a real skewed router.

Reading: MXFP8 is the faster fso path at every M, by 8–18 % over BSFP8. That
gap is glue, not GEMM — the dense tables in [`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md)
show the two dtypes running the same kernel within a timer step, and the
difference here is the fused silu-and-quantize stage the MXFP8 block has and
the BSFP8 block does not. Against the fused cuBLAS chain the MXFP8 block is
1.47–1.78× faster through the decode band (M ≤ 64) and 1.07–1.15× from
M = 128 up; against the unfused cuBLAS chain it is 3.7–5.4× faster at every M,
which is the price of the reference quantize rather than a property of cuBLAS's
GEMM. Against the BF16 reference the block goes from 1.79× at M = 1 to 2.11× at
M = 8192. Two shapes of the grid are worth naming. The MXFP8 column barely
moves from M = 1 to M = 16 — 18.48, 18.48, 18.49, 18.48 and 18.50 µs for a
sixteenfold growth in token count — and steps up one replay tick at M = 32,
because in that band the block is bound by its chain of kernel launches rather
than by arithmetic; both of its GEMMs are served there by the decode row of
[`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md) (`gate_up` by the persistent kernel,
`down` by the in-cluster split-K kernel), and the column reads the same
18.48–18.50 µs the previous run recorded. From this run the row also serves
`gate_up` at M = 64 with its 64-wide token tile, while `down` — long in K and
narrow in N — stays with the C++ cascade's two-kernel split-K there, and the
M = 64 cell reads as it did (26.68 against 26.69 µs): the row's saving on the
isolated `gate_up` cell does not shorten the block. The dense wave-tile rule
owns the block's `gate_up` at M = 128, where the cell is routed by how many
CTAs a narrower N tile puts on the 148 SMs. And the M = 4096 and M = 8192 rows of every column are
the ones the unlocked clock reaches — read M = 4096 with the ±3 % band rather
than ±1 %, and read M = 8192 with the wider band the scatter measurement in
[`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md) established, which is about 8 % and
applies to the pure-cuBLAS comparator column as well as to the fso ones.

## Family B — Qwen3-30B-A3B routed MoE layer (E=128, top-8, moe_inter 768)

fso: masked-slab MXFP8 layer (`m_cap` rows per expert). From this run the
captured graph is five kernels at every M — the routing builder, the
gather-quantize, the two grouped GEMMs and the weighted combine — because
both of the stages a grouped GEMM used to need ahead of it are absorbed on
both routes. In the decode band, where few enough experts can hold a row for
the slot-bound route to be taken, a grouped GEMM consumes the packed
active-expert list that the routing kernel emits in the launch that already
computes the histogram it is a compaction of; on the pointer-array cascade it
consumes the per-group `(rows, N, K)` problem shapes the same routing kernel
writes in the pass that publishes the per-expert counts, so the
argument-preparation kernel that used to run ahead of each pointer-array GEMM
is gone (the routing-independent arrays are written once per tensor set,
outside the graph). The fused SwiGLU epilogue on FC1 removes the separate
SwiGLU-and-quantize kernel on both routes. Which route each GEMM takes is
decided per kernel: on this family both GEMMs are on the slot route to M = 32,
FC2 alone stays on it to M = 64 (the plain slot kernel runs to its full
64-wide token tile, the fused-SwiGLU slot kernel to one 32-column epilogue
chunk), and from M = 128 both are on the cascade. The layer asks the library
per GEMM which of the two lists that GEMM will read and requests exactly that
one from the routing kernel. Counted with nsys on this run, the graph holds
five kernels per replay at M = 1, 8, 32, 48, 64, 1024 and 4096 (the previous
run's held seven from M = 64 up).
`model ms` = µs × 48 / 1000. M ≤ 4096 for the masked slab layout; M = 96 is an
extra measured row.

Reading: fso is ahead of both torch comparators at every M where it has a cell.
Against `scaled_grouped_mm` in MXFP8 the margin is 4.7–4.9× at M = 1–2 and
2.1–2.8× at M = 4–8, narrows to 1.7–1.9× through M = 16–512 where both
implementations are bandwidth-shaped, and widens again to 2.2–2.5× from
M = 1024 up. Against `_grouped_mm` in BF16 — which does no quantization at all
and reads twice the weight bytes — the margin is 4.1–4.5× at M = 1–2 and stays
between 2.1× and 3.0× from M = 4 up. The fso block itself is nearly flat from
M = 32 to M = 256 (103.4 → 129.4 µs for an eightfold token count), because in
that band the layer is paying for the weights of the active experts rather than
for arithmetic; `weight GB/s` peaks at 4852 and falls to 2140 by M = 4096, which
is where the slab stops fitting in L2 between replays. The two ends of the grid
are where the MXFP8 round's work shows, and they show for different reasons. At
M = 1–2 almost nothing in the block is GEMM, so what the layer costs is the
chain of small kernels; the slot-bound route removes two of them outright,
because each grouped GEMM is indexed by the packed expert list the routing
kernel already produced and needs no argument-preparation launch of its own,
and from this run its FC1 carries the fused SwiGLU epilogue as well, so the
separate SwiGLU-and-quantize kernel is gone there too and the captured graph is
five kernels. At M ≥ 1024 the two stages that grow with the token count are the
activation quantize and the FC1 output, and both were folded into a neighbour:
each token is quantized once and scattered to its top-k destinations instead of
being quantized once per destination, and FC1's SwiGLU and requantize are done
in the grouped GEMM's own epilogue rather than in a kernel that first writes a
BF16 intermediate. Between those ends, the M = 64–1024 cells moved in this run
for two reasons: the pointer-array cascade no longer runs an
argument-preparation kernel ahead of each grouped GEMM, because the routing
kernel supplies the per-group problem shapes, and at M = 64 the FC2 stays on
the slot route while the FC1 takes the fused pointer-array kernel (121.6 µs
against the previous run's 123.5 at M = 64, 129.4 against 133.8 at M = 256,
149.3 against 152.7 at M = 1024). At M = 4096 the cell reads 282.2 µs against
279.6: with the preparation kernels gone the GEMM's early-launched CTAs wait
behind a longer predecessor under programmatic dependent launch, a movement
inside the ±3 % band this device needs at that M. The M = 8192 row has no fso
cell: the `G × m_cap` masked slab does not fit, the same limit sm_120 has.

Reading: on the routed layer fso is 1.7–6.3× faster than `scaled_grouped_mm`
MXFP8 and 2.2–4.6× faster than `_grouped_mm` BF16 across the whole grid, and
the routed + shared block holds a narrower but similar lead against its own
comparators (against `scaled_grouped_mm`, 4.1× at M = 1 and 2.2× at M = 4096).
The shared expert costs a flat 10.8–17.7 µs from M = 1 to M = 1024 — its two
GEMMs are small enough to sit at the replay floor, as the dense rows in
[`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md) show — and only starts to scale above
that: 24.2 µs at M = 2048 and 35.3 µs at M = 4096, which is 11 % of the block
there. The two torch columns cross over:
BF16 is the slower comparator through the middle of the grid but the faster one
at M = 1–4 and again at M = 4096, because its per-call overhead is smaller at
decode and its extra weight traffic only starts to bite once the block is
bandwidth-bound. What separates this block from the 2026-09-17 run's is the
same mechanisms Family B shows, and they apply here unchanged even though this
family's `moe.gate_up` is a different shape: the slot-bound route at the decode
end with the fused SwiGLU epilogue on its FC1, the quantize-once gather, the
fused SwiGLU epilogue on the pointer-array FC1 from M = 64 upward, the
partitioned direct-store epilogue (its `moe.down` kernel cell is the one that
changed pick in the previous run; see the grouped-kernel table in
[`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md)), and from this run the
routing-supplied problem shapes on the cascade, which is what moved the
M = 64–2048 cells here (138.3 µs against the previous run's 140.5 at M = 64,
183.5 against 187.5 at M = 1024), and the per-kernel slot clause, which takes
the fused FC1 off the slot route from m_cap = 36 and so removes a loss the
previous rule had at M = 40–56 (measured off the published grid in the run
directory). With 256 experts the FC2 leaves the slot route at M = 32 already
(its live tiles exceed fourteen waves of the machine), so this family is on
the cascade for both GEMMs from M = 64. As in Family B the M = 8192 fso cells
are absent for the masked-slab size limit.
