All runs: the B300 SXM6 AC at PCI `06:00.0` of pod `fish-dev-5f7c8f8bbf-crb2m`,
**clocks unlocked** (no lock is available on this pod), every cell preceded by
`FSO_BENCH_WARM_MS=300` and taken under a `flock` that keeps one process on the
device. Driver 595.84, torch 2.11.0+cu130, CUDA 13.0, CUTLASS 4.4.2, built
`ARCH=10.0f`; commit `cc4b443` plus the uncommitted B300 change set, squashed
as **this commit**. The full environment table, the mechanisms this run added
(the dense wave-tile rule; in the MoE layer the split prep kernel with its
programmatic dependent launch, the multi-CTA routing builder and grouped
cascade v2), the unlocked-device caveats (about ±3 % at M ≥ 4096, the wider
scatter of the M = 8192 MLP-block cells, clock droop at M = 8192, the ~2.05 µs
replay tick) and the L2 caveat are in [`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md);
they apply to every row here.

Every comparator row on this page was measured on the same card, in the same
session and on the same tree as the fso rows it sits next to, with the same
warm-up and the same graph-replay protocol. Family A's two cuBLAS columns are
cells of the same bench pass as its fso columns and share that pass's inputs.
The MoE comparators are separate passes, run at the end of the same session
under the same GPU lock, driven by the same routing generator and seed as the
fso passes, so both sides see the same expert assignment at every M. To bound
how much those comparator columns move on their own, the three torch files were
re-measured at M ∈ {1, 64, 1024} after the full pass: every one of those
eighteen cells agrees with the published row to within 0.5 %.

The first tables on this page were measured on 2026-09-15 on a different card
of the same pod (PCI `86:00.0`, UUID `GPU-b2bdc48e-…`) and were replaced
wholesale rather than merged with the run above, because rows from two cards
are not comparable under this protocol.

Comparators (allowed only in this directory, [`../../README.md`](../../README.md) rule 2):

| column | what it is | version | caveat |
|---|---|---|---|

| torch _grouped_mm BF16 | `torch._grouped_mm` with BF16 weights, same block | torch 2.11.0+cu130 | a speed reference, not a competitor: it skips both quantize steps and reads two bytes per weight element instead of one |

Two structural notes on the MoE comparators. First, neither sglang nor
deep_gemm is installed on this pod, so the comparator columns here are not the
ones `sm90.md` and `sm120.md` carry; a column of this file is not comparable to
a column of those. Second, the torch rows and the fso rows share the routing
generator and its seed, so they see the same expert assignment, but the torch
layer computes exactly `M · 8` rows of GEMM while the masked-slab layout
computes `E · m_cap` rows and discards the padding — the two are comparable as
time per layer, not as arithmetic done per layer. The routing itself is uniform
(`randperm(E)[:8]` per token), which is easier for every grouped implementation
than a real skewed router.

Reading: MXFP8 is the faster fso path at every M, by 7–15 % over BSFP8. That
gap is glue, not GEMM — the dense tables in [`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md)
show the two dtypes running the same kernel within a timer step, and the
difference here is the fused silu-and-quantize stage the MXFP8 block has and
the BSFP8 block does not. Against the fused cuBLAS chain the MXFP8 block is
1.23–1.43× faster through the decode band (M ≤ 64) and a steady 1.09–1.15× from
M = 128 up; against the unfused cuBLAS chain it is 3.5–4.4× faster at every M,
which is the price of the reference quantize rather than a property of cuBLAS's
GEMM. Against the BF16 reference the block goes from 1.31× at M = 1 to 2.16× at
M = 8192. Two shapes of the grid are worth naming. The MXFP8 column barely
moves from M = 1 to M = 32 — 26.50, 26.68, 25.29, 24.70, 24.62 and 26.07 µs for
a thirty-two-fold growth in token count — because in that band the block is
bound by its chain of kernel launches and by the tile granularity rather than
by arithmetic; the dense wave-tile rule described in
[`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md) starts only at M = 64, so the M = 64 and
M = 128 cells are the two where the block's `gate_up` is routed by how many
CTAs a narrower N tile puts on the 148 SMs. And the M = 4096 and M = 8192 rows of every column are
the ones the unlocked clock reaches — read M = 4096 with the ±3 % band rather
than ±1 %, and read M = 8192 with the wider band the scatter measurement in
[`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md) established, which is about 8 % and
applies to the pure-cuBLAS comparator column as well as to the fso ones.

Reading: fso is ahead of both torch comparators at every M where it has a cell.
Against `scaled_grouped_mm` in MXFP8 the margin is about 2.5× at M = 1–2 and
1.7–1.8× at M = 4–8, narrows to about 1.6× through M = 16–512 where both
implementations are bandwidth-shaped, and widens again to 1.7–2.0× from
M = 1024 up. Against `_grouped_mm` in BF16 — which does no quantization at all
and reads twice the weight bytes — the margin stays close to 2× everywhere up
to M = 2048 and falls to 1.6× at M = 4096. The fso block itself is nearly flat from M = 32 to M = 256
(117.8 → 147.6 µs for an eightfold token count), because in that band the layer
is paying for the weights of the active experts rather than for arithmetic;
`weight GB/s` peaks at 4410 and falls to 1627 by M = 4096, which is where the
slab stops fitting in L2 between replays. The two ends of the grid are where
this run's glue work shows, and they show for different reasons. At M = 1–2
almost nothing in the block is GEMM, so what the layer costs is the chain of
small kernels; the split prep kernel and the programmatic dependent launches
overlap part of that chain with the kernel ahead of it. At M ≥ 1024 the routing
builder is the part that grows with the token count, and from 4096 routed pairs
— M = 512 at top-8 — it runs on many CTAs instead of one, which is why the
block's growth from M = 1024 to M = 4096 is flatter than the token count. The
M = 8192 row has no fso cell: the `G × m_cap` masked slab does not fit, the
same limit sm_120 has.

Reading: on the routed layer fso is 1.5–2.8× faster than `scaled_grouped_mm`
MXFP8 and 1.7–2.3× faster than `_grouped_mm` BF16 across the whole grid, and
the same ratios hold for the routed + shared block against its own comparators
(2.3× at M = 1, 1.7× at M = 4096). The shared expert costs a flat 13.6–18.6 µs
from M = 1 to M = 1024 — its two GEMMs are small enough to sit at the replay
floor, as the dense rows in [`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md) show — and
only starts to scale above that: 23.8 µs at M = 2048 and 37.5 µs at M = 4096,
which is 9 % of the block there. The two torch columns cross over: BF16 is the
slower comparator through the middle of the grid but the faster one at M = 1–4
and again at M = 4096, because its per-call overhead is smaller at decode and
its extra weight traffic only starts to bite once the block is bandwidth-bound.
Cascade v2's new grouped tile does not fire in this family — `moe.gate_up`'s
N = 1024 is not a multiple of 192 — so what separates this block from the
previous run's is the glue: the split prep kernel and the dependent launches at
the decode end, and the multi-CTA routing builder from M = 512 upward. As in
Family B the M = 8192 fso cells are absent for the masked-slab size limit.
