All runs: the B300 SXM6 AC at PCI `E6:00.0` of pod `fish-dev-5f7c8f8bbf-hlvt4`,
**clocks unlocked** (no lock is available on this pod), every cell preceded by
`FSO_BENCH_WARM_MS=300` and taken under a `flock` that keeps one process on the
device. Driver 595.84, torch 2.11.0+cu130, CUDA 13.0, CUTLASS 4.4.2, built
`ARCH=10.0f`; commit `72516f4` plus the uncommitted MXFP8 MoE change set,
squashed as **this commit**. The full environment table, the mechanisms this
run added (in the MoE layer the slot-bound decode route with its per-tile dead
slot skip, the packed active-expert list emitted by the routing kernel, the
quantize-once gather, and the fused SwiGLU epilogue on FC1; on the dense path
the equal-CTA tie-break in the wave-tile rule), the unlocked-device caveats
(about ±3 % at M ≥ 4096, the wider scatter of the M = 8192 MLP-block cells,
clock droop at M = 8192, the ~2.05 µs replay tick) and the L2 caveat are in
[`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md); they apply to every row here.

Every comparator row on this page was measured on the same card, in the same
session and on the same tree as the fso rows it sits next to, with the same
warm-up and the same graph-replay protocol. Family A's two cuBLAS columns are
cells of the same bench pass as its fso columns and share that pass's inputs.
The MoE comparators are separate passes, run at the end of the same session
under the same GPU lock, driven by the same routing generator and seed as the
fso passes, so both sides see the same expert assignment at every M. To bound
how much those comparator columns move on their own, all three torch files were
measured a second time in the same session: over the eighty-four cells, the
median pass-to-pass difference is under half a per cent.

The tables on this page have now been measured on three different cards of the
same pod — `86:00.0` on 2026-09-15, `06:00.0` on 2026-09-17 and `E6:00.0` here
— and each run replaced its predecessor wholesale rather than being merged with
it, because rows from two cards are not comparable under this protocol. The
card in use is not a free variable: the comparator columns of this page, which
contain no fso kernel and cannot move with a library change, read about 1.8 %
faster here than the same columns of the 2026-09-17 run, so a number of this
page differs from that run's by roughly that much before any code difference is
counted.

Two structural notes on the MoE comparators. First, neither sglang nor
deep_gemm is installed on this pod, so the comparator columns here are not the
ones `sm90.md` and `sm120.md` carry; a column of this file is not comparable to
a column of those. Second, the torch rows and the fso rows share the routing
generator and its seed, so they see the same expert assignment, but the torch
layer computes exactly `M · 8` rows of GEMM while the masked-slab layout
computes `E · m_cap` rows and discards the padding — the two are comparable as
time per layer, not as arithmetic done per layer. The routing itself is uniform
(`randperm(E)[:8]` per token), which is easier for every grouped implementation
than a real skewed router.

Reading: MXFP8 is the faster fso path at every M, by 8–25 % over BSFP8. That
gap is glue, not GEMM — the dense tables in [`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md)
show the two dtypes running the same kernel within a timer step, and the
difference here is the fused silu-and-quantize stage the MXFP8 block has and
the BSFP8 block does not. Against the fused cuBLAS chain the MXFP8 block is
1.31–1.47× faster through the decode band (M ≤ 64) and a steady 1.09–1.15× from
M = 128 up; against the unfused cuBLAS chain it is 3.7–4.4× faster at every M,
which is the price of the reference quantize rather than a property of cuBLAS's
GEMM. Against the BF16 reference the block goes from 1.34× at M = 1 to 2.12× at
M = 8192. Two shapes of the grid are worth naming. The MXFP8 column barely
moves from M = 1 to M = 32 — 24.63, 25.06, 24.67, 24.82, 24.50 and 24.82 µs for
a thirty-two-fold growth in token count — because in that band the block is
bound by its chain of kernel launches and by the tile granularity rather than
by arithmetic; the dense wave-tile rule described in
[`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md) starts only at M = 64, so the M = 64 and
M = 128 cells are the two where the block's `gate_up` is routed by how many
CTAs a narrower N tile puts on the 148 SMs. And the M = 4096 and M = 8192 rows of every column are
the ones the unlocked clock reaches — read M = 4096 with the ±3 % band rather
than ±1 %, and read M = 8192 with the wider band the scatter measurement in
[`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md) established, which is about 8 % and
applies to the pure-cuBLAS comparator column as well as to the fso ones.

## Family B — Qwen3-30B-A3B routed MoE layer (E=128, top-8, moe_inter 768)

fso: masked-slab MXFP8 layer (`m_cap` rows per expert). How many kernels the
captured graph holds now depends on the band, because two of the layer's
stages are taken over by a grouped GEMM that does their work in its own
launch. In the decode band, where few enough experts can hold a row for the
slot-bound route to be taken, each grouped GEMM consumes the packed
active-expert list that the routing kernel already emits, so neither GEMM
needs an argument-preparation kernel ahead of it and the graph is six kernels:
the routing builder, the gather-quantize, the two grouped GEMMs, the
SwiGLU-and-quantize, and the weighted combine. Above that band the dispatcher
returns to the pointer-array cascade, which does need one argument-preparation
kernel per grouped GEMM, but there the first grouped GEMM carries a fused
SwiGLU epilogue that emits the quantized FC1 output directly, so the separate
SwiGLU-and-quantize kernel disappears instead and the graph is seven kernels.
Counted with nsys on this run, the graph holds six kernels per replay at
M = 1 and M = 8 and seven at M = 64, M = 1024 and M = 4096.
`model ms` = µs × 48 / 1000. M ≤ 4096 for the masked slab layout; M = 96 is an
extra measured row.

Reading: fso is ahead of both torch comparators at every M where it has a cell.
Against `scaled_grouped_mm` in MXFP8 the margin is 4.3–4.7× at M = 1–2 and
2.1–2.7× at M = 4–8, narrows to 1.6–1.8× through M = 16–512 where both
implementations are bandwidth-shaped, and widens again to 2.3× from
M = 1024 up. Against `_grouped_mm` in BF16 — which does no quantization at all
and reads twice the weight bytes — the margin stays between 2.1× and 2.9×
across the whole grid. The fso block itself is nearly flat from M = 32 to M = 256
(104.3 → 138.9 µs for an eightfold token count), because in that band the layer
is paying for the weights of the active experts rather than for arithmetic;
`weight GB/s` peaks at 4796 and falls to 2164 by M = 4096, which is where the
slab stops fitting in L2 between replays. The two ends of the grid are where
this run's work shows, and they show for different reasons. At M = 1–2
almost nothing in the block is GEMM, so what the layer costs is the chain of
small kernels; the slot-bound route removes two of them outright, because each
grouped GEMM is indexed by the packed expert list the routing kernel already
produced and needs no argument-preparation launch of its own. At M ≥ 1024 the
two stages that grow with the token count are the activation quantize and the
FC1 output, and both were folded into a neighbour: each token is quantized once
and scattered to its top-k destinations instead of being quantized once per
destination, and FC1's SwiGLU and requantize are done in the grouped GEMM's own
epilogue rather than in a kernel that first writes a BF16 intermediate. The
M = 8192 row has no fso cell: the `G × m_cap` masked slab does not fit, the
same limit sm_120 has.

Reading: on the routed layer fso is 1.6–5.5× faster than `scaled_grouped_mm`
MXFP8 and 2.2–4.3× faster than `_grouped_mm` BF16 across the whole grid, and
the routed + shared block holds a narrower but similar lead against its own
comparators (3.9× at M = 1, 2.1× at M = 4096). The shared expert costs a flat
12.3–17.7 µs from M = 1 to M = 1024 — its two GEMMs are small enough to sit at
the replay floor, as the dense rows in [`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md)
show — and only starts to scale above that: 24.6 µs at M = 2048 and 39.0 µs at
M = 4096, which is 12 % of the block there. The two torch columns cross over:
BF16 is the slower comparator through the middle of the grid but the faster one
at M = 1–4 and again at M = 4096, because its per-call overhead is smaller at
decode and its extra weight traffic only starts to bite once the block is
bandwidth-bound. What separates this block from the previous run's is the same
three mechanisms Family B shows, and they apply here unchanged even though this
family's `moe.gate_up` is a different shape: the slot-bound route at the decode
end, the quantize-once gather, and the fused SwiGLU epilogue on FC1 from
M = 64 upward. As in Family B the M = 8192 fso cells are absent for the
masked-slab size limit.
