Two structural notes on the MoE comparators. First, the comparator set of
this page is torch-native: the third-party columns that `sm90.md` and
`sm120.md` carry (sglang and deep_gemm on sm_90; sglang, vLLM, TensorRT-LLM
and FlashInfer on sm_120) were not run on the B300, so a column of this file
is not comparable to a column of those. Second, the torch rows and the fso rows share the routing
generator and its seed, so they see the same expert assignment, but the torch
layer computes exactly `M · 8` rows of GEMM while the masked-slab layout
computes `E · m_cap` rows and discards the padding — the two are comparable as
time per layer, not as arithmetic done per layer. The routing itself is uniform
(`randperm(E)[:8]` per token), which is easier for every grouped implementation
than a real skewed router.

Reading (cold weights, 2026-10-01 release run): MXFP8 is the faster fso path
at every M, by 11–13 % over BSFP8 up to M = 512 and by 7–17 % from M = 1024
up, where the power cap scatters the cells. That gap is glue, not GEMM — the
dense tables in [`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md) show the two dtypes
running the same kernel within 0.1 µs of each other up to M = 512, and the
difference here is the fused silu-and-quantize stage the MXFP8 block has and
the BSFP8 block does not. Against the fused cuBLAS chain the MXFP8 block is
1.41–1.49× faster through the decode band (M ≤ 64) except at M = 32 (1.31×),
and 1.04–1.16× from M = 128 up; against the unfused cuBLAS chain it is
3.3–4.2× faster at every M, which is the price of the reference quantize
rather than a property of cuBLAS's GEMM. Against the BF16 reference the block
is 1.23–1.97× faster (1.34× at M = 1 and 1.95× at M = 8192). With the weights
streamed from HBM the block's decode band is not flat at the replay floor: the
MXFP8 column reads 24.4 / 24.7 / 25.1 / 25.2 / 26.1 µs from M = 1 to 16 (the
2026-09-23 warm tables read 18.5 µs at every one of those M), of which about
12 µs is the 75 MB of `gate_up` and `down` weights at the ~6.2 TB/s streaming
ceiling and the rest the chain of kernel launches. Both GEMMs are served there
by the decode row of [`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md), and the row also
serves `gate_up` at M = 64 with its 64-wide token tile while `down` stays with
the C++ cascade's split-K there (the block reads 32.1 µs at M = 64). The dense
wave-tile rule owns the block's `gate_up` at M = 128. The M ≥ 1024 rows of
every column are the ones the unlocked clock reaches under the power cap: the
dense prefill cells of this run differ by up to 21 % between its two passes, so
read them with that band rather than ±1 %; it applies to the pure-cuBLAS
comparator columns as well as to the fso ones.

## Family B — Qwen3-30B-A3B routed MoE layer (E=128, top-8, moe_inter 768)

fso: masked-slab MXFP8 layer (`m_cap` rows per expert). Since the 2026-09-23
run the captured graph is five kernels at every M — the routing builder, the
gather-quantize, the two grouped GEMMs and the weighted combine — because
both of the stages a grouped GEMM used to need ahead of it are absorbed on
both routes. In the decode band, where few enough experts can hold a row for
the slot-bound route to be taken, a grouped GEMM consumes the packed
active-expert list that the routing kernel emits in the launch that already
computes the histogram it is a compaction of; on the pointer-array cascade it
consumes the per-group `(rows, N, K)` problem shapes the same routing kernel
writes in the pass that publishes the per-expert counts, so the
argument-preparation kernel that used to run ahead of each pointer-array GEMM
is gone (the routing-independent arrays are written once per tensor set,
outside the graph). The fused SwiGLU epilogue on FC1 removes the separate
SwiGLU-and-quantize kernel on both routes. Which route each GEMM takes is
decided per kernel: on this family both GEMMs are on the slot route to M = 32,
FC2 alone stays on it to M = 64 (the plain slot kernel runs to its full
64-wide token tile, the fused-SwiGLU slot kernel to one 32-column epilogue
chunk), and from M = 128 both are on the cascade. The layer asks the library
per GEMM which of the two lists that GEMM will read and requests exactly that
one from the routing kernel. Counted with nsys on the 2026-09-23 run, the
graph held five kernels per replay at M = 1, 8, 32, 48, 64, 1024 and 4096 (the
2026-09-22 run's held seven from M = 64 up).
`model ms` = µs × 48 / 1000. Since the 0.2.0 release the bench's fit test
covers sm_100/103 as well, so the fso rows run to M = 8192
([`README.md`](README.md)); M = 96 is an extra measured row.

Reading (cold weights, 2026-10-01 release run): fso is ahead of both torch
comparators at every M. Against `scaled_grouped_mm` in MXFP8 the margin is
4.6× / 4.0× at M = 1 / 2 (17.2 / 25.4 µs against 79.8 / 100.6), 2.7× / 2.2×
at M = 4 / 8, narrows to 1.7–1.9× through M = 16–512 where both
implementations are bandwidth-shaped, and widens again to 2.1–2.5× from
M = 1024 up. Against `_grouped_mm` in BF16 — which does no quantization at all
and reads twice the weight bytes — the margin is 3.8× / 3.6× at M = 1 / 2 and
stays between 2.0× and 2.9× from M = 4 up. With the expert weights streamed
from HBM the decode band is not served from the L2: M = 2 reads 25.4 µs where
the 2026-09-23 warm table read 20.5, because the 16 active experts' 75 MB come
from HBM on every copy, while M = 1 (17.2 µs against the warm 16.0) moves less
because its 37.7 MB had streamed at one copy already. The round-2 combine
change (its routing-input loads before the PDL wait) was worth −2.2 % on the
M = 1 cell in the library A/B of 2026-09-29. The fso block itself is nearly
flat from M = 32 to M = 256 (102.6 → 129.6 µs for an eightfold token count),
because in that band the layer is paying for the weights of the active experts
rather than for arithmetic; `weight GB/s` peaks at 4876 at M = 32 and falls to
2209 by M = 4096 and 1244 at M = 8192, where the slab's own traffic takes
over. The mechanisms behind the two ends of the grid are the 2026-09-23
session's (the slot-bound decode route with the fused SwiGLU FC1, the
quantize-once gather, the fused SwiGLU epilogue on the pointer-array FC1, the
routing-supplied problem shapes) and are described in that session's notes
above; the release build compiles them to the same machine code. Every busy
clock sample of the fso MoE files read 2032 MHz apart from three in the
Family C files (the power cap acts on the dense tables, `../gemm/sm100.md`).
The M = 8192 row is new in this release: fso reads 485.6 µs there, 2.10×
ahead of `scaled_grouped_mm` and 2.03× ahead of BF16.

Reading (cold weights, 2026-10-01 release run): on the routed layer fso is
1.7–5.8× faster than `scaled_grouped_mm` MXFP8 (5.8× / 5.3× at M = 1 / 2,
3.4× / 2.5× at M = 4 / 8, 1.7–1.9× from 16 to 512, 2.2–2.4× from 1024 up) and
2.1–4.2× faster than `_grouped_mm` BF16 across the whole grid, and the routed +
shared block holds a narrower but similar lead against its own comparators
(against `scaled_grouped_mm`, 3.9× at M = 1, 1.6–1.8× through the middle band,
2.1× at M = 4096 and 2.0× at M = 8192; against BF16 2.8× / 2.7× at M = 1 / 2
and 1.9–2.6× above). The shared expert costs a flat 13–18 µs from M = 1 to
M = 1024 — its two GEMMs are small enough to sit at the replay floor, as the
dense rows in [`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md) show — and only starts to
scale above that: 23.0 µs at M = 2048, 36.3 µs at M = 4096 (11 % of the block
there) and 73.7 µs at M = 8192 (14 %). The two torch columns cross over: BF16
is the slower comparator through the middle of the grid but the faster one at
M = 1–4 and again at M = 4096 and 8192, because its per-call overhead is
smaller at decode and its extra weight traffic only starts to bite once the
block is bandwidth-bound. Cold weights moved the small-M cells the same way as
in Family B (M = 1 15.8 µs against the 2026-09-23 warm 14.4, M = 2 20.1
against 18.5, M = 4 29.0 against 25.9: the active experts' weights come from
HBM on every copy), and the combine change of round 2 was worth −2.1 % at
M = 1 in the library A/B of 2026-09-29. The mechanisms of this block are the
2026-09-23 session's, compiled to the same machine code in the release build:
the slot-bound route at the decode end with the fused SwiGLU epilogue on its
FC1, the quantize-once gather, the fused SwiGLU epilogue on the pointer-array
FC1 from M = 64 upward, the partitioned direct-store epilogue and the
routing-supplied problem shapes on the cascade; with 256 experts the FC2 leaves
the slot route at M = 32 already (its live tiles exceed fourteen waves of the
machine), so this family is on the cascade for both GEMMs from M = 64. The
M = 8192 row is new in this release (437.2 µs for the routed layer and
510.9 µs for the routed + shared block), and like every other MoE cell of this
run it ran at the sustained clock apart from three clock samples.
