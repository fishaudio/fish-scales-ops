Two structural notes on the MoE comparators. First, neither sglang nor
deep_gemm is installed on this pod, so the comparator columns here are not the
ones `sm90.md` and `sm120.md` carry; a column of this file is not comparable to
a column of those. Second, the torch rows and the fso rows share the routing
generator and its seed, so they see the same expert assignment, but the torch
layer computes exactly `M · 8` rows of GEMM while the masked-slab layout
computes `E · m_cap` rows and discards the padding — the two are comparable as
time per layer, not as arithmetic done per layer. The routing itself is uniform
(`randperm(E)[:8]` per token), which is easier for every grouped implementation
than a real skewed router.

Reading (cold weights, 2026-09-29): MXFP8 is the faster fso path at every M,
by 7–13 % over BSFP8. That gap is glue, not GEMM — the dense tables in
[`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md) show the two dtypes running the same
kernel within a timer step, and the difference here is the fused
silu-and-quantize stage the MXFP8 block has and the BSFP8 block does not.
Against the fused cuBLAS chain the MXFP8 block is 1.43–1.47× faster through
the decode band (M ≤ 64) and 1.06–1.15× from M = 128 up; against the unfused
cuBLAS chain it is 3.4–4.2× faster at every M, which is the price of the
reference quantize rather than a property of cuBLAS's GEMM. Against the BF16
reference the block goes from 1.34× at M = 1 to 1.95× at M = 8192. With the
weights streamed from HBM the block's decode band is no longer flat at the
replay floor: the MXFP8 column reads 24.4 / 24.6 / 25.0 / 25.0 / 26.0 µs from
M = 1 to 16 (the 2026-09-23 warm tables read 18.5 µs at every one of those
M), of which about 12 µs is the 75 MB of `gate_up` and `down` weights at the
~6.2 TB/s streaming ceiling and the rest the chain of kernel launches; both
GEMMs are served there by the decode row of
[`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md), and the row also serves `gate_up` at
M = 64 with its 64-wide token tile while `down` stays with the C++ cascade's
split-K there (32.0 µs at M = 64). The dense wave-tile rule owns the block's
`gate_up` at M = 128. The M ≥ 1024 rows of every column are the ones the
unlocked clock reaches under the power cap: two passes of this run differ by
up to 12 % there, so read them with that band rather than ±1 %; it applies to
the pure-cuBLAS comparator columns as well as to the fso ones.

## Family B — Qwen3-30B-A3B routed MoE layer (E=128, top-8, moe_inter 768)

fso: masked-slab MXFP8 layer (`m_cap` rows per expert). From this run the
captured graph is five kernels at every M — the routing builder, the
gather-quantize, the two grouped GEMMs and the weighted combine — because
both of the stages a grouped GEMM used to need ahead of it are absorbed on
both routes. In the decode band, where few enough experts can hold a row for
the slot-bound route to be taken, a grouped GEMM consumes the packed
active-expert list that the routing kernel emits in the launch that already
computes the histogram it is a compaction of; on the pointer-array cascade it
consumes the per-group `(rows, N, K)` problem shapes the same routing kernel
writes in the pass that publishes the per-expert counts, so the
argument-preparation kernel that used to run ahead of each pointer-array GEMM
is gone (the routing-independent arrays are written once per tensor set,
outside the graph). The fused SwiGLU epilogue on FC1 removes the separate
SwiGLU-and-quantize kernel on both routes. Which route each GEMM takes is
decided per kernel: on this family both GEMMs are on the slot route to M = 32,
FC2 alone stays on it to M = 64 (the plain slot kernel runs to its full
64-wide token tile, the fused-SwiGLU slot kernel to one 32-column epilogue
chunk), and from M = 128 both are on the cascade. The layer asks the library
per GEMM which of the two lists that GEMM will read and requests exactly that
one from the routing kernel. Counted with nsys on this run, the graph holds
five kernels per replay at M = 1, 8, 32, 48, 64, 1024 and 4096 (the previous
run's held seven from M = 64 up).
`model ms` = µs × 48 / 1000. M ≤ 4096 for the masked slab layout; M = 96 is an
extra measured row.

Reading (cold weights, 2026-09-29): fso is ahead of both torch comparators at
every M where it has a cell. Against `scaled_grouped_mm` in MXFP8 the margin
is 4.8× / 4.1× at M = 1 / 2 (17.2 / 25.1 µs against 83.1 / 103.2), 2.7× / 2.3×
at M = 4 / 8, narrows to 1.7–1.9× through M = 16–512 where both
implementations are bandwidth-shaped, and widens again to 2.2–2.5× from
M = 1024 up. Against `_grouped_mm` in BF16 — which does no quantization at all
and reads twice the weight bytes — the margin is 4.0× / 3.7× at M = 1 / 2 and
stays between 2.2× and 3.0× from M = 4 up. With the expert weights streamed
from HBM the decode band is no longer served from the L2: M = 2 reads 25.1 µs
where the 2026-09-23 warm table read 20.5, because the 16 active experts'
75 MB now come from HBM on every copy, while M = 1 (17.2 against 16.0) moves
less because its 37.7 MB had streamed at one copy already; the round-2 combine
change (its routing-input loads before the PDL wait) is worth −2.2 % on that
cell against the previous build. The fso block itself is nearly flat from
M = 32 to M = 256 (102.6 → 130.2 µs for an eightfold token count), because in
that band the layer is paying for the weights of the active experts rather
than for arithmetic; `weight GB/s` peaks at 4877 at M = 32 and falls to 2130 by
M = 4096, where the slab's own traffic takes over. The mechanisms behind the
two ends of the grid are the 2026-09-23 session's (the slot-bound decode
route with the fused SwiGLU FC1, the quantize-once gather, the fused SwiGLU
epilogue on the pointer-array FC1, the routing-supplied problem shapes) and
are described in that session's notes above; they are unchanged in this
build. The M = 4096 row is under the power cap on this device (two passes of
this run differ by up to 12 % there), and the M = 8192 row has no fso cell:
the `G × m_cap` masked slab does not fit, the same limit sm_120 has.

M = 4 / 8, 1.7–2.0× from 16 to 512, 2.2–2.4× from 1024 up) and 2.2–4.3× faster
than `_grouped_mm` BF16 across the whole grid, and the routed + shared block
holds a narrower but similar lead against its own comparators (against
`scaled_grouped_mm`, 4.0× at M = 1, 1.6–1.8× through the middle band and 2.2×
at M = 4096; against BF16 2.9× at M = 1 and 2.1–2.6× above). The shared expert
costs a flat 13–17 µs from M = 1 to M = 1024 — its two GEMMs are small enough
to sit at the replay floor, as the dense rows in
[`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md) show — and only starts to scale above
that: 23.0 µs at M = 2048 and 36.4 µs at M = 4096, which is 11 % of the block
there. The two torch columns cross over: BF16 is the slower comparator
through the middle of the grid but the faster one at M = 1–4 and again at
M = 4096, because its per-call overhead is smaller at decode and its extra
weight traffic only starts to bite once the block is bandwidth-bound. Cold
weights moved the small-M cells the same way as in Family B (M = 1 16.0 µs
against the warm 14.4, M = 2 20.2 against 18.5, M = 4 28.6 against 25.9: the
active experts' weights now come from HBM on every copy) and the combine
change of round 2 is worth −2.1 % at M = 1. The mechanisms of this block are
the 2026-09-23 session's, unchanged in this build: the slot-bound route at the
decode end with the fused SwiGLU epilogue on its FC1, the quantize-once
gather, the fused SwiGLU epilogue on the pointer-array FC1 from M = 64 upward,
the partitioned direct-store epilogue and the routing-supplied problem shapes
on the cascade; with 256 experts the FC2 leaves the slot route at M = 32
already (its live tiles exceed fourteen waves of the machine), so this family
is on the cascade for both GEMMs from M = 64. As in Family B the M = 8192 fso
cells are absent for the masked-slab size limit, and the M = 4096 cells carry
the power-cap band.
