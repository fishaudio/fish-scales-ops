## Comparators

Every comparator row on this page was measured in the same run as the fso rows
it sits next to (`final-0.2.0-b300`; the generated block gives the run and
window of every file), so on the same card, on the same day and under the same
cold-weight protocol. Family A's two cuBLAS columns and its BF16 column are
cells of the same bench pass as its fso columns, from the same worker process,
and share that pass's inputs; its sglang column comes from the last step of the
run, which started 32 minutes after the block's fso pass.
The MoE comparators are separate passes driven by the same routing generator and
seed as the fso passes, so both sides see the same expert assignment at every M.
They are later steps of the same run, under the same GPU lock and one pass per
library and family: the two torch columns of the routed layer, then sglang's
Triton layers, then trtllm-gen, then the two torch columns of the routed +
shared block, each starting 19 to 40 minutes after the fso pass of its family.
All but fifteen of the 1119 busy clock samples of the six routed-layer
comparator passes read 2032 MHz; the fifteen are in the torch and sglang passes
at M ≥ 128, fourteen of them carry the software power-cap flag, and the lowest
reads 1710 MHz (torch, Family B, M = 8192). The trtllm-gen passes read 2032 MHz
throughout. The routed + shared comparator pass has four busy samples below
2032 MHz, at M = 128, 256, 512 and 8192, all with the flag, the lowest 1920 MHz.

Two structural notes on the MoE comparators. First, the comparator columns
differ per device: this page carries TensorRT-LLM's trtllm-gen fused MoE,
sglang's Triton fused MoE and two torch-native layers, `sm90.md` carries
sglang's Triton layers and the deep_gemm masked pipeline, and `sm120.md` carries
vLLM's and sglang's Triton layers, TensorRT-LLM's CUTLASS fused MoE and torch's
`_grouped_mm`, so a column of this file is not comparable to a column of those.
Second, every comparator row shares the routing generator and its seed with the
fso rows, so both see the same expert assignment, but the torch layer computes
exactly `M · 8` rows of GEMM while the masked-slab layout computes `E · m_cap`
rows and discards the padding — the two are comparable as time per layer, not as
arithmetic done per layer. The routing itself is uniform (`randperm(E)[:8]` per
token), which is easier for every grouped implementation than a real
skewed router.

The TRT-LLM trtllm-gen column is TensorRT-LLM's fused MoE in its DeepSeek
block-FP8 mode (1×128 activation groups, 128×128 weight blocks, fp32 scales) as
FlashInfer ships it: the op
`flashinfer.fused_moe.trtllm_fp8_block_scale_routed_moe`, whose two batched
GEMMs are the precompiled trtllm-gen cubins of the `flashinfer-cubin` wheel and
whose routing, activation and weighted-combine kernels are TensorRT-LLM's own.
It is the op apex serves a block-FP8 MoE with on this card when fso is off and
the experts are not split across devices, and the bench makes the call apex
makes: the top-k ids and weights are given, sglang's per-token-group-128
activation quantize runs inside the captured graph in front of the op, and the
weights stay in the plain K-major layout that apex and sglang use. FlashInfer
also offers a shuffled, block-major weight layout for this op that gives the
same results and read slightly faster in the 2026-09-16 study cited below; the
column keeps the plain layout because that is the layout apex and sglang
serve with.

Three caveats come with the column. First, the kernel treats the first half of
each expert's gate/up weight as the up projection and applies the SiLU to the
second half, the opposite of the `[gate; up]` order of fso and of the bench's
reference, so the bench stacks the two halves as `[up; gate]` once, when it
quantizes the weights, as the sglang and apex weight loaders do for this op. A
call that passes `[gate; up]` silently computes `silu(up) · gate` and still
reads a plausible cos of about 0.990. Second, the kernel rounds the FC1 output
to FP8 before the SwiGLU and rounds the SwiGLU output to FP8 again for FC2, one
FP8 rounding more than the fso layer makes, so its cos against the bench's
reference reads 0.9973–0.9975 in Family B (0.9968 at M = 1) and 0.9973–0.9974 in
Family C, below the fso column's cos at every M. That is the kernel's own
accuracy floor rather than a calling error; every cell records its cos in the
jsonl, and no cell is dropped for it. Third, the kernel's tactics are autotuned
for each M before the graph is captured, which apex does not do; the 2026-09-16
study of this op (`/data/bench-runs/b300_thirdparty_20260916/G4/REPORT.md`)
found the tuned tactic faster than the default one at M = 1024 and no different
at M = 1, 64 and 4096, the other token counts it compared. The op has no
shared-expert path, so the Family C table carries this column for the routed
layer only, and the routed + shared block has no trtllm-gen column.

## Family A — Qwen3-4B dense MLP block

`gate_up` (N=19456, K=2560) → silu·mul → `down` (N=2560, K=9728), activation
quantize inside the graph. MXFP8 uses the fused
`silu_chunk_mul_quantize_1x32_fp8` (no BF16 intermediate); BSFP8 uses the
1×128 quantize and a `torch.compile`d silu·mul. `model ms` = µs × 36 layers / 1000.

The sglang column runs the same block with the BSFP8 column's block-FP8 recipe
(1×128 activation scales, 128×128 weight scales): sglang's block-FP8 linear,
described under Comparators (cuBLAS's block-FP8 `scaled_mm` is n/a on this arch,
and the B300 environment has no vLLM). It is read from
`tests/baselines/ref_mlp_qwen3_4b_b300.jsonl`. Its title names the GEMM backend
its cells recorded, and its ×fso BSFP8 and ×fso MXFP8 columns are its µs
divided by the BSFP8 and the MXFP8 µs of the same row, so a value above 1 means
fso is ahead. On this card `fso.dense` serves a block-FP8 checkpoint through the
MXFP8 column's path, requantizing the weights to MXFP8 1×32 at load
([`../../api/dense.md`](../../api/dense.md)), so ×fso MXFP8 is the ratio a
caller of `fso.dense` gets; the reading below sets the sglang column against both
fso columns.

Reading (cold weights): MXFP8 is the faster fso path at every M, by 10–13 %
over BSFP8 up to M = 512 and by 9–10 % from M = 1024 up. That gap is glue, not
GEMM — the dense tables in [`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md) show the two
dtypes running the same kernel within 0.1 µs of each other up to M = 512, and
the difference here is the fused silu-and-quantize stage the MXFP8 block has
and the BSFP8 block does not. Against the fused cuBLAS chain the MXFP8 block is
1.42–1.49× faster from M = 1 to 64 except at M = 32 (1.30×), and 1.05–1.15×
from M = 128 up; against the unfused cuBLAS chain it is 3.3–4.2× faster at
every M, which is the price of the reference quantize rather than a property
of cuBLAS's GEMM. Against the BF16 reference the block is 1.23–1.94× faster:
1.35× at M = 1, least at M = 64 (1.23×) and most at M = 8192 (1.94×). With the
weights streamed from HBM the block's decode band is not flat at the replay
floor: the MXFP8 column reads 24.25 / 24.72 / 24.89 / 25.06 / 26.14 µs from
M = 1 to 16 (the 2026-09-23 warm tables read 18.5 µs at every one of those M),
of which about 12 µs is the 75 MB of `gate_up` and `down` weights at the
~6.2 TB/s streaming ceiling and the rest the chain of kernel launches. Both
GEMMs are served there by the decode row of
[`../gemm/sm100.md`](../gemm/sm100.md), and the row also serves `gate_up` at
M = 64 with its 64-wide token tile while `down` stays with the C++ cascade's
split-K there (the block reads 32.02 µs at M = 64). The dense wave-tile rule
owns the block's `gate_up` at M = 128. The M ≥ 1024 rows of every column are
the ones the unlocked clock reaches under the power cap (in this run the
block's fso pass carries the flag from M = 1024 and reads below 2032 MHz from
M = 2048), and in the 2026-10-01 release run the dense prefill cells differed
by up to 21 % between two passes, so read them with that band rather than
±1 %; it applies to the pure-cuBLAS comparator columns as well as to the fso
ones.

Against sglang's block-FP8 linear (DeepGEMM on this arch, with PDL on), fso's
MXFP8 block — the path `fso.dense` serves a block-FP8 checkpoint through, and
the table's ×fso MXFP8 column — is ahead 1.06–1.14× from M = 1 to 16 (24.25
against 27.69 µs at M = 1), behind it from M = 32 to 2048 by 6.1 %, 12.0 %,
12.5 %, 6.2 %, 1.0 %, 4.1 % and 5.0 % (34.77 against 30.43 µs at M = 128), and
ahead 1.03× and 1.04× at M = 4096 and 8192; the geomeans are 1.02× over the
decode band and 0.98× over prefill. fso's BSFP8 block, the table's ×fso BSFP8
column, is ahead of sglang's only at M = 1 (1.02×), level at M = 2 (1.00×) and
behind it from M = 4 up, by 0.9–21.7 % (geomeans 0.91× and 0.89×). sglang's
cells read cos 0.9966–0.9969 against fso's 0.9978–0.9979, the cost of its
power-of-two scales (Comparators). The sglang column comes from the last step
of the same run, 32 minutes after the block's fso pass; both passes held
2032 MHz below M = 2048, and from M = 2048 up both have busy samples below it,
most under the power cap, so its ratios at M ≥ 2048 carry the pass-to-pass
scatter described above.

## Family B — Qwen3-30B-A3B routed MoE layer (E=128, top-8, moe_inter 768)

fso: masked-slab MXFP8 layer (`m_cap` rows per expert). Since the 2026-09-23
run the captured graph is five kernels at every M — the routing builder, the
gather-quantize, the two grouped GEMMs and the weighted combine — because
both of the stages a grouped GEMM used to need ahead of it are absorbed on
both routes. In the decode band, where few enough experts can hold a row for
the slot-bound route to be taken, a grouped GEMM consumes the packed
active-expert list that the routing kernel emits in the launch that already
computes the histogram it is a compaction of; on the pointer-array cascade it
consumes the per-group `(rows, N, K)` problem shapes the same routing kernel
writes in the pass that publishes the per-expert counts, so the
argument-preparation kernel that used to run ahead of each pointer-array GEMM
is gone (the routing-independent arrays are written once per tensor set,
outside the graph). The fused SwiGLU epilogue on FC1 removes the separate
SwiGLU-and-quantize kernel on both routes. Which route each GEMM takes is
decided per kernel: on this family both GEMMs are on the slot route to M = 32,
FC2 alone stays on it to M = 64 (the plain slot kernel runs to its full
64-wide token tile, the fused-SwiGLU slot kernel to one 32-column epilogue
chunk), and from M = 128 both are on the cascade. The layer asks the library
per GEMM which of the two lists that GEMM will read and requests exactly that
one from the routing kernel. Counted with nsys on the 2026-09-23 run, the
graph held five kernels per replay at M = 1, 8, 32, 48, 64, 1024 and 4096 (the
2026-09-22 run's held seven from M = 64 up).
`model ms` = µs × 48 / 1000. Since the 0.2.0 release the bench's fit test
covers sm_100/103 as well, so the fso rows run to M = 8192
([`README.md`](README.md)); M = 96 is an extra measured row.
