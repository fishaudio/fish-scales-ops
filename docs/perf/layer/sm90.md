# Layer performance — sm_90 (NVIDIA H200)

Whole MLP / MoE block per shape family as one CUDA-graph replay. Block
contents, FLOPs and the `model ms` convention: [`README.md`](README.md);
protocol, clock policy and M grids: [`../README.md`](../README.md). Tables are
generated from `tests/baselines/` jsonl — do not hand-edit rows.

## Comparators

Comparators (allowed only in this directory, `../../README.md` rule 2):

| column | what it is | caveat |
|---|---|---|
| sglang block-FP8 linear (Family A) | sglang's dense block-FP8 MLP as its Qwen3 MLP runs it: `Fp8LinearMethod.apply` for `gate_up` and for `down`, each with sglang's per-token-group-128 activation quantize inside the graph, and `SiluAndMul` between them. The weights are loaded the way a block-FP8 checkpoint stores them (FP8 E4M3 with fp32 128×128 block scales) and post-processed by sglang's `process_weights_after_loading` | sglang's default `--fp8-gemm-backend auto` runs DeepGEMM (the `sgl-deep-gemm` package) on sm_90 through `deepgemm_w8a8_block_fp8_linear_with_fallback`. Both projections meet its N % 64 and K % 128 rule, so its Triton fallback never runs, and the block is five kernels: sglang's per-token-group quantize, DeepGEMM's `sm90_fp8_gemm_1d2d` GEMM, sglang's `act_and_mul`, the quantize again and the GEMM again. The weight scales stay fp32. DeepGEMM chooses its tile for each M with its own heuristic, with no tuned config file, and JIT-compiles it on first use; the bench turns off sglang's start-up pre-compile of every M, which only fills the compile cache earlier and changes no kernel choice. The cell also makes the start-up call every sglang TP worker makes, `update_deep_gemm_config`, which turns DeepGEMM's programmatic dependent launch (PDL) on (`SGLANG_DEEPGEMM_PDL`, on by default in sglang 0.5.20), so DeepGEMM runs with PDL on as sglang serves it; every row records that state (`deepgemm_pdl`, true in all of them) |
| cuBLAS scaled_mm block-FP8 (Family A) | torch's `F.scaled_mm` with BlockWise1x128 activation scales and BlockWise128x128 weight scales (fp32), which runs cuBLASLt's block-scaled FP8 GEMM, on the same block-FP8 weights; the 1×128 activation quantize and the silu·mul are `torch.compile`d functions inside the graph | cuBLASLt's own heuristic picks the GEMM kernel (`nvjet_sm90_qqtst_*` in the kernel trace); at M = 1 and 2 it splits the `down` GEMM along K, which adds a `splitKreduce` kernel to the block. cuBLASLt has no block-scaled algorithm when M is not a multiple of 4, so the cell zero-pads the activation rows to the next multiple of 4 inside the graph: the M = 1 and M = 2 rows compute four rows. torch accepts this scaling recipe on sm_90 only |

## Family A — Qwen3-4B dense MLP block

`gate_up` (N=19456, K=2560) → silu·mul → `down` (N=2560, K=9728), activation
quantize inside the graph; BSFP8 uses a `torch.compile`d silu·mul followed by
the 1×128 quantize (no fused dense silu-quantize on sm_90). `model ms` =
µs × 36 layers / 1000.

The serving-library columns run the same block with the same block-FP8 recipe
(1×128 activation scales, 128×128 weight scales): sglang's block-FP8 linear and
cuBLAS's block-FP8 `scaled_mm`, described under Comparators. They are read from

them against.

Reading (cold weights, natural clock; ×fso BSFP8 as defined above). sglang's
block-FP8 linear, which runs DeepGEMM's `sm90_fp8_gemm_1d2d` kernel with PDL on,
is faster than fso's BSFP8 block at every M. Its lead is 5.3–7.4 % at M = 1, 2
and 4 (40.65 against 38.50 µs at M = 1) and 14.2–18.8 % from M = 8 to 128 (48.04
against 39.01 µs at M = 128), and over prefill it is 6.8–18.8 %, most at
M = 1024 (173.71 against 141.12 µs); the geomeans are 0.87× over the decode band
and 0.88× over prefill. cuBLAS's block-FP8 `scaled_mm` is slower than fso's
block through the decode band (1.02–1.39×, geomean 1.12×) and from M = 256 to
1024 (1.03–1.19×), and faster from M = 2048 up, by 2.9 %, 2.3 % and 4.4 %. Every
cell of the three columns reads cos 0.9978–0.9980. Both runs held 1980 MHz
through the decode band; from M = 256 up in the fso pass, and from M = 1024 up
in the comparator passes, busy samples fall below it, most of them under the
700 W power cap (`../gemm/sm90.md`, Environment), so the prefill ratios compare
two power-capped passes taken about seven hours apart.

## Family B — Qwen3-30B-A3B routed MoE layer (E=128, top-8, moe_inter 768)

fso: `moe_layer_fp8_sm90`, expert-sorted contiguous BSFP8 in five kernels on
every route: the routing kernel, which builds the sorted layout on device; the
gather-quantize; the FC1 (`gate_up`), with the SwiGLU and the 1×128 FP8
requantize fused into its epilogue; the FC2 (`down`); and the weighted combine.
The route follows the routed rows per expert (M · top-k / E). Up to 12 rows both
GEMMs run swap-AB with a 16-row activation tile, and up to 24 rows with a
32-row tile. Above that the FC1 runs non-swap on 64-row blocks, and the FC2
runs the swap-AB GEMM with a 64-row tile on the same layout, whose output is
bit-identical to the non-swap FC2. On this family that is the 16-row tile up
to M = 192, the 32-row tile up to M = 384 and the non-swap FC1 above it (on
Family C, M = 384 and M = 768); the `path` column reads `contiguous` for the
rows with the non-swap FC1. The fused swap-AB FC1 runs one CTA per SM, with the
largest stage count that fits shared memory and divides K/128. `model ms` =
µs × 48 / 1000. M = 96 is an extra measured row.

Reading (cold weights, natural clock; ×fso = comparator µs / fso µs, so > 1 is
fso ahead, as `perf_report.py report` prints it for this run): sglang's Triton
layers run apex's tuned Triton configs (Comparators names the file each GEMM
resolved). sglang's Triton FP8 w8a8-block layer is the fastest comparator at
every M of both families, and fso is ahead of it at every M but one: 1.11× at
M = 1 (21.9 against 24.2 µs), 1.07× at M = 2, 1.10× at M = 8, 1.02–1.03× from 16
to 128, 1.10× at M = 256, 1.02–1.04× at M = 512 and 1024, 1.07× at M = 2048 and
1.11× at 4096 and 8192 (1200.7 against 1330.1 µs at 8192). The exception is
M = 4, where fso is behind at 0.97× (47.7 against 46.5 µs). The geomean is 1.04×
over the decode band (M = 1–128) and 1.08× over prefill (M > 128). sglang's
Triton BF16 layer is 1.45–2.47× slower than fso (geomean 1.69× decode, 2.11×
prefill; twice the weight bytes). sglang's production deep_gemm masked pipeline,
which the bench runs on the decode band only and with PDL on as sglang serves
it, is 2.53× slower at M = 1 (55.3 µs) and 1.30× at M = 128 (214.1 against
164.1; geomean 1.58×); its per-expert padding to the masked block size and its
glue kernels are what it pays at small M.

## History

These notes describe tables and runs before the 0.2.0 release wheel; numbers
quoted in them are not the current tables.

**The 2026-10-01 MoE sections (Families B and C).** The two MoE tables, including the
routed + shared block, were re-measured on 2026-10-01 after the 0.2.0 change
to the sm_90 MoE layer (the fused FC1 on every route and the route rule given
under Family B). They ran in one chain from 07:26:33 to 07:42:24 UTC, run
`/data/bench-runs/h200_moe_tables_20261001/`, on GPU 6 of the local eight-H200
box `39-gcp-h200-2` (PCI `CB:00.0`, `GPU-f7556d6e-d480-6de1-0a17-1a04bafb2c91`,
pinned by UUID), at the card's natural clock (`../README.md` §5) and under the
cold-weight protocol (`../README.md` §1: R copies of the weights with
R × bytes ≥ 2 × the 60 MB L2 in one captured graph, copy cap 256;
µs = replay / R). The fso rows and every comparator ran in one environment,
the apex-0520 venv: torch 2.13.0+cu130, triton 3.7.1, sglang 0.5.20 and
deep_gemm 0.2.0, with the sm_90 kernels JIT-compiled in-process by the venv's
NVRTC 13.0 (a same-session probe shows fso and deep_gemm both loading it). The
code was the fish-scales-ops 0.2.0 release code, measured from the release tree
just before its commit; the release build (commit `633f5ca`) re-measured the
three MoE files in the same environment during the release perf run of
2026-10-01 (10:29–10:42 UTC) and matched these tables within ±1 % in all 45
cells (file medians +0.01 % to +0.04 %), so the tables were kept. A sampler
read the clock every
200 ms: every file ran at a median of 1980 MHz, and the 80 samples (of 4721
busy ones) that carried the SW power-cap flag came in bursts of one to five at
the heavy prefill cells, dipping to 1545–1875 MHz. Every MoE row and every
comparator column of this page came from that one session, so they were
comparable with each other. The per-section times that the status table then
gave were the windows of the run's per-file clock records.

**Family A (the dense MLP block), 2026-10-01.** Its row, like every table of
[`../gemm/sm90.md`](../gemm/sm90.md), came from the fish-scales-ops 0.2.0
release perf run of 2026-10-01 (run
`/data/bench-runs/fso_release_perf_20261001/h200/`, report `REPORT_stage1.md`,

**Family C against sglang's Triton FP8 layer, 2026-10-01 tables.** In the
2026-10-01 tables fso was at parity with that layer over the decode band
(geomean 1.00×) and slightly behind over prefill (geomean 0.98×). In the
2026-09-29 tables fso had led by 1.17× over the decode band and trailed by 0.99×
over prefill. The measurement report attributes the change to the comparator,
not to fso: fso's own rows moved −0.7 % and −2.9 % at M = 2048 and 4096 against
those tables, while sglang 0.5.20's Triton kernel got faster than 0.5.14's (its
Family C FP8 layer by 8–22 % in the decode band).

**The routed + shared block at M = 1, 2026-10-01 tables.** At decode the
routed + shared block read 35.6 µs at M = 1, 1.0 µs more than
the 34.6 µs of the 2026-09-29 table, while the routed layer alone was unchanged
at 19.5 µs. The table's shared-expert cost is the block minus the routed layer,
two graphs measured separately, so it showed that 1.0 µs as shared-expert time
(15.1 to 16.1 µs) even where the change sits on the routed side. A
same-environment A/B on 2026-10-01 settles where it sits. It ran the pristine
537efa5 package against the 0.2.0 tree, both arms in apex-0520 on GPU 6, and
its data is in `/data/bench-runs/h200_moe_tables_20261001/shared_ab/`.
- **The code change, about 0.55 µs.** Inside the block the fused swap-AB FC1
  runs 0.36 µs longer than it does in the routed layer alone, and the gap
  after it grows by about 0.13 µs. The shared expert's own kernels are
  unchanged (+0.05 µs).
- **The toolchain change, an estimated 0.4 µs.** This is torch 2.13 and
  NVRTC 13.0 against torch 2.11 and NVRTC 13.2.78.
- **The ratio against 537efa5.** In that A/B the block is 1.0155× 537efa5 at
  M = 1, the one cell of this release outside the ±1 % bar, and
  0.976–0.990× from M = 2 to 32.

The extra time at M = 1 comes from the fused FC1's first read of the expert-id
table. That read takes longer when the shared expert's kernels run between two
invocations of the FC1. It also depends on where the intermediate buffers are
placed: five placements gave 1.006–1.015×, and the default placement was the
worst. The 0.2.0 release accepts this cell. Finding the mechanism (address
translation or L2 placement) is open kernel work.

**What changed against the 2026-09-29 MoE tables (history).** The previous MoE
rows came from the 2026-09-29 run `h200_unlocked_20260929`: the fso rows under the
`.dev` interpreter (torch 2.11, NVRTC 13.2.78) and the comparators under the
tiny_sglang `sgl` venv (sglang 0.5.14, triton 3.6.0, deep_gemm 0.1.3), with the
kernel set of the 2026-09-25 decode tuning described below. Against those rows
the fso files moved by −1.4 % to −2.2 % in median, and Family B by −10 % to
−12 % at M ≥ 2048, but that comparison mixes the code change with the
toolchain change. The clean measure of the code change is the same-environment
A/B of the route retune (both arms under apex-0520, 38 cells of both families,
run `/data/bench-runs/sm90_retune_20261001/`): the 0.2.0 code was 1.4–11.8 %
faster than `537efa5` at 35 cells and within ±1 % at the other three (Family C
M = 1, 384 and 3072), with bit-identical outputs. The comparators moved with
their new versions: the deep_gemm masked pipeline is 14–36 % faster, sglang's
Family C Triton BF16 layer 19–30 % faster in prefill (it now runs a tuned
sglang config on that shape), and sglang's Family C Triton FP8 layer 8–22 %
faster in the decode band.

Why the section was re-baselined then, and the one cell that was unsettled.
The rows it replaced had been measured on 2026-09-03 at commit `85d9c31` on a
different host, and one of them had drifted away from what this box produced:
the M = 4096 BSFP8 cell held 665.63 µs, while the 2026-09-22 session measured
the same cell four times — twice on `5add318` and twice on its parent
`72516f4`, which changes no sm_90 dense code — and got 616.89, 674.45, 617.91
and 658.15 µs. The old row was therefore not a number that box reproduced for
either commit, and the new one was not a settled number either: the four
readings are bimodal with a 9.3 % spread, the two modes appear on both
commits, and the 2026-09-22 row (616.89 µs, 992 TFLOPS, 22.21 model ms) was
the lowest of the four because the convention is to publish pass 1 of two.
The neighbouring M = 2048 and M = 8192 cells of the same passes agreed to
better than 1.1 %, so this was a property of that one cell rather than of the
bench. The row of that cell in the 2026-10-01 tables (699.67 µs) came from the 2026-10-01
release run at the natural clock and carried the power-cap caveat of
`../gemm/sm90.md`; do not accept or reject a change on that cell alone. The per-cell numbers of
2026-09-22 are in that run directory's `delta_tables_noise.txt`, table N3.
