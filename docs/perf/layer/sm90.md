# Layer performance — sm_90 (NVIDIA H200)

Whole MLP / MoE block per shape family as one CUDA-graph replay. Block
contents, FLOPs and the `model ms` convention: [`README.md`](README.md);
protocol, clock policy and M grids: [`../README.md`](../README.md). Tables are
generated from `tests/baselines/` jsonl — do not hand-edit rows.

| sglang block-FP8 linear (Family A) | sglang's dense block-FP8 MLP as its Qwen3 MLP runs it: `Fp8LinearMethod.apply` for `gate_up` and for `down`, each with sglang's per-token-group-128 activation quantize inside the graph, and `SiluAndMul` between them. The weights are loaded the way a block-FP8 checkpoint stores them (FP8 E4M3 with fp32 128×128 block scales) and post-processed by sglang's `process_weights_after_loading` | sglang's default `--fp8-gemm-backend auto` runs DeepGEMM (the `sgl-deep-gemm` package) on sm_90 through `deepgemm_w8a8_block_fp8_linear_with_fallback`. Both projections meet its N % 64 and K % 128 rule, so its Triton fallback never runs, and the block is five kernels: sglang's per-token-group quantize, DeepGEMM's `sm90_fp8_gemm_1d2d` GEMM, sglang's `act_and_mul`, the quantize again and the GEMM again. The weight scales stay fp32. DeepGEMM chooses its tile for each M with its own heuristic, with no tuned config file, and JIT-compiles it on first use; the bench turns off sglang's start-up pre-compile of every M, which only fills the compile cache earlier and changes no kernel choice. ## Family A — Qwen3-4B dense MLP block

`gate_up` (N=19456, K=2560) → silu·mul → `down` (N=2560, K=9728), activation
quantize inside the graph; BSFP8 uses a `torch.compile`d silu·mul followed by
the 1×128 quantize (no fused dense silu-quantize on sm_90). `model ms` =
µs × 36 layers / 1000.

The serving-library columns run the same block with the same block-FP8 recipe
(1×128 activation scales, 128×128 weight scales): sglang's block-FP8 linear and
cuBLAS's block-FP8 `scaled_mm`, described under Comparators. They are read from
`tests/baselines/ref_mlp_qwen3_4b_h200.jsonl`, measured in a separate run on the
same card about 5.7 hours before the fso rows. Each column title names the GEMM
backend its cells recorded, and each comparator has a ×fso BSFP8 column: its µs
divided by the BSFP8 µs of the same row, so a value above 1 means fso is ahead.
On this card `fso.dense` serves a block-FP8 checkpoint through the BSFP8 path
([`../../api/dense.md`](../../api/dense.md)), so BSFP8 is the fso column to read
them against.

Reading (cold weights, natural clock; ×fso BSFP8 as defined above). fso's BSFP8
block is ahead of sglang's block-FP8 linear, which runs DeepGEMM's
`sm90_fp8_gemm_1d2d` kernel with PDL on, from M = 1 to 32: 1.16–1.18× at M = 1,
2 and 4 (32.61 against 38.50 µs at M = 1) and 1.05–1.07× from M = 8 to 32.
sglang's linear is faster at M = 64 and 128, by 4.9 % and 5.6 % (41.31 against
39.01 µs at M = 128), and the geomean over the decode band is 1.07×. Over
prefill the lead changes hands: fso is ahead at M = 512 and 4096 (1.09× and
1.02×), and sglang's linear is faster at M = 256 by 1.6 %, at M = 1024 by 10.5 %
(157.72 against 141.12 µs) and at M = 2048 and 8192 by 6.3 % and 6.0 %; the
prefill geomean is 0.97×. cuBLAS's block-FP8 `scaled_mm` is slower than fso's
block at every M: 1.27–1.62× through the decode band (geomean 1.38×) and
1.02–1.34× over prefill (geomean 1.14×), closest at M = 2048. Every cell of the
three columns reads cos 0.9978–0.9980. Both runs held 1980 MHz through the
decode band; from M = 512 up in the fso pass, and from M = 1024 up in the
comparator passes, busy samples fall below it, most of them under the 700 W
power cap (`../gemm/sm90.md`, Environment), so the prefill ratios compare two
power-capped passes taken about 5.7 hours apart.

## Family B — Qwen3-30B-A3B routed MoE layer (E=128, top-8, moe_inter 768)

fso: `moe_layer_fp8_sm90`, expert-sorted contiguous BSFP8 in five kernels on
every route: the routing kernel, which builds the sorted layout on device; the
gather-quantize; the FC1 (`gate_up`), with the SwiGLU and the 1×128 FP8
requantize fused into its epilogue; the FC2 (`down`); and the weighted combine.
The route follows the routed rows per expert (M · top-k / E). Up to 12 rows both
GEMMs run swap-AB with a 16-row activation tile, and up to 24 rows with a
32-row tile. Above that the FC1 runs non-swap on 64-row blocks, and the FC2
runs the swap-AB GEMM with a 64-row tile on the same layout, whose output is
bit-identical to the non-swap FC2. On this family that is the 16-row tile up
to M = 192, the 32-row tile up to M = 384 and the non-swap FC1 above it (on
Family C, M = 384 and M = 768); the `path` column reads `contiguous` for the
rows with the non-swap FC1. The fused swap-AB FC1 runs one CTA per SM, with the
largest stage count that fits shared memory and divides K/128; when one CTA per
SwiGLU block would occupy at most a quarter of the SMs (on these two families,
Family C at M = 1 only), two CTAs of a cluster share each block (History).
`model ms` = µs × 48 / 1000. M = 96 is an extra measured row.

Reading (cold weights, natural clock; ×fso = comparator µs / fso µs, so > 1 is
fso ahead, as `perf_report.py report` prints it from the installed baselines):
sglang's Triton layers run apex's tuned Triton configs (Comparators names the
file each GEMM resolved). sglang's Triton FP8 w8a8-block layer is the fastest
comparator at every M of both families, and fso is ahead of it at every M but
one: 1.11× at M = 1 (21.9 against 24.2 µs), 1.07× at M = 2, 1.10× at M = 8,
1.02–1.04× from 16 to 128, 1.09–1.13× from M = 256 to 1024 and 1.21–1.26× from

fso is behind at 0.98× (47.6 against 46.5 µs). The geomean is 1.04× over the
decode band (M = 1–128) and 1.17× over prefill (M > 128). sglang's Triton BF16
layer is 1.45–2.82× slower than fso (geomean 1.69× decode, 2.29× prefill; twice
the weight bytes). sglang's production deep_gemm masked pipeline, which the
bench runs on the decode band only and with PDL on as sglang serves it, is
2.53× slower at M = 1 (55.3 µs) and 1.32× at M = 128 (214.1 against 162.7;
geomean 1.58×); its per-expert padding to the masked block size and its glue
kernels are what it pays at small M.

package. On 2026-10-01 a same-environment A/B had measured it 1.55 % slower than
`537efa5` (History), and the 0.2.0 candidate listed that as a known issue. On
2026-10-05 the same A/B harness ran the final 0.2.0 wheel against the pristine
`537efa5` package, both in the apex-0520 environment on H200 GPU 1, in four
rounds of alternating order
(`/data/bench-runs/fso_release_0.2.0_20261005/shared_m1_ab/`, `REPORT.md`). The
block took 30.04 µs against 34.85 µs at M = 1, a ratio of 0.862, and it was
faster at every M the harness measures (0.862–0.941 from M = 1 to 32); the
outputs were bit-identical. That A/B compares the two packages as they are
served, so the gain combines three changes: the split fused FC1 of the routed
experts, the faster dense GEMM chain of the shared expert, and the bundled NVRTC
13.2.78 in place of the NVRTC 13.0.88 that the older package binds through
torch. The routed layer alone read 0.951 of `537efa5` at M = 1 in the same A/B.
Whether the fused FC1 on its own still runs slower inside the block than in the
routed-only graph, which was the mechanism of the 2026-10-01 result, was not
measured again.

## History

These notes describe tables and runs before the final 0.2.0 wheel; numbers
quoted in them are not the current tables.

**The sm_90 kernel round, 2026-10-05 (history).** Every fso row of this page
was re-measured from the final 0.2.0 wheel (commit `5253cd4`) in the run
`final-0.2.0-h200`, 18:05–18:18 UTC; the comparator rows were not re-measured.
The fso rows they replaced had been installed at commit `6bc45a4` from the run
`release-0.2.0-wheel-h200`, 05:19–05:34 UTC the same day, which measured the
first 0.2.0 wheel (built at `37e316b`). Both runs used the same card (GPU 0,
PCI `8F:00.0`) and the same environment. Between the two wheels three commits
changed the sm_90 kernels, and the outputs of the dense GEMMs and of the MoE
layer are bitwise identical to before:
- `12fc87d` and `24b2a1f` changed the dense path that the Family A block and the
  shared expert of Family C run: a swizzled TMA output store in the GEMM, new
  tile rules, a 1×128 activation quantize that gives each 128-element group to
  eight lanes, and PDL between the quantize and the GEMM (`../gemm/sm90.md`,
  History, describes them).
- `1eb550d` changed the MoE layer in three places. Above 2048 routed pairs the
  routing kernel, which builds the expert-sorted layout, gives each warp its own
  counters, where every thread of its single CTA used to update one shared
  histogram. From 32 tokens on, the sorted gather-quantize reads and quantizes
  each token row once and writes it to all of the token's routed rows, where it
  used to do that once per routed pair. When one CTA per SwiGLU block of the
  fused swap-AB FC1 would occupy at most a quarter of the SMs, two CTAs of a
  cluster share each block and exchange the per-token maxima that the 1×128
  requantize needs; on these two families that is Family C at M = 1 only.
