generated from `tests/baselines/` jsonl — do not hand-edit rows.

Why the section was re-baselined, and the one cell that is still unsettled.
The rows it replaces were measured on 2026-09-03 at commit `85d9c31` on a
different host, and one of them had drifted away from what this box now
produces: the M = 4096 BSFP8 cell held 665.63 µs, while the 2026-09-22 session
measured the same cell four times — twice on `5add318` and twice on its parent
`72516f4`, which changes no sm_90 dense code — and got 616.89, 674.45, 617.91
and 658.15 µs. The old row is therefore not a number this box reproduces for
either commit. It is equally true that the new row is not a settled number:
those four readings are bimodal with a 9.3 % spread, the two modes appear on
both commits, and the published value is the lowest of the four because the
convention is to publish pass 1 of two. The neighbouring M = 2048 and
M = 8192 cells of the same passes agree to better than 1.1 %, so this is a
property of that one cell rather than of the bench. Read the M = 4096 BSFP8
row, and the 992 TFLOPS and 22.21 model-ms figures derived from it, as the
fast end of a range that reaches about 674 µs, and do not accept or reject a
change on that cell alone. The per-cell numbers are in that run directory's
`delta_tables_noise.txt`, table N3.

Reading (cold weights, natural clock; ×fso = comparator µs / fso µs, so > 1 is
fso ahead): against sglang's Triton FP8 w8a8-block layer fso is ahead 1.22×
at M = 1 (22.3 against 27.2 µs), 1.13× / 1.04× / 1.08× at M = 2 / 4 / 8, level
at M = 16–128 (1.00–1.03×) and at M = 256 (1.06×), and behind from M = 512 up
(0.95–0.99×: 267.7 against 258.1 µs at M = 1024, 1332.3 against 1300.5 at
8192, the non-swap block_m = 64 contiguous path); geomean 1.07× over the decode
band (M = 1–128) and 0.99× over prefill (M > 128). sglang's Triton BF16 layer
is 1.40–1.83× slower than fso at every M ≤ 1024 and 1.15–1.26× at 2048–8192
(twice the weight bytes); sglang's production deep_gemm masked pipeline, which
the bench runs on the decode band only, is 3.9× slower at M = 1 (86.5 µs) and
still 1.53× at M = 128 (257.2 against 167.9), its per-expert padding to the
masked block size and its five glue kernels being what it pays at small M.
