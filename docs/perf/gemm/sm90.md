# GEMM performance — sm_90 (NVIDIA H200)

Frozen reference numbers for the block-scaled GEMM paths on sm_90. Protocol,
FLOPs conventions, shape families, M grids and clock policy: [`../README.md`](../README.md).
Tables are generated from `tests/baselines/` jsonl — do not hand-edit rows.

## Environment

| field | value |
|---|---|
| device | NVIDIA H200, 132 SMs |
| clock | **natural clock, no lock**, the datacenter-card policy of `../README.md` §5, with a 200 ms clock sampler beside the run (`../README.md` §7c). The power cap on the dense prefill rows is described below |
| kernels | the vendored deep_gemm block-FP8 kernels, NVRTC-JIT-compiled in-process at first call; since `9cf0a46` by the NVRTC that fish-scales-ops bundles, not the one torch loads. The NVRTC build is part of the result (`../README.md` §8) |

The card, the driver, the clocks the sampler observed, the package versions
(torch and the cuBLAS behind the BF16 column among them), the fish-scales-ops
build and the sm_90 JIT compiler of the run behind each baseline file are not
repeated here: the generated block
[Environments of record](../README.md#environments-of-record) shows them,
rendered from each run's manifest.

Cold weights (all tables in this file since 2026-09-29): the captured graph
rotates through R copies of the weight (R × bytes ≥ 2 × the 60 MB L2, up to
256 copies for the smallest projections) and reports replay time / R, so no
row is served from L2; under the earlier single-copy replay the M ≤ 128 rows
had been (10–50 MB of weights fit the L2) and their µs undercut the HBM floor
(`../README.md` §1 and §8). The rotation also amortises the ~2 µs
`cudaGraphLaunch` gap that protocol charged to every cell, so the sub-4 µs
cells read lower than the warm tables did. Since the sm_90 kernel round of
2026-10-05 the dense GEMM is launched with programmatic dependent launch (PDL).
In the rotation graph each GEMM therefore launches, and runs its prologue,
while the GEMM before it finishes. In a model the kernel before an fso GEMM is
its activation quantize, which lets the GEMM launch early in the same way, so
there the GEMM overlaps the quantize instead; a GEMM that follows a kernel
without this launch chaining gets no such overlap. History gives the size of
this effect on the per-GEMM rows, and the MLP block of `../layer/sm90.md`
measures the chain as a model runs it.

Power cap on the dense prefill rows. At its natural clock the card runs every
decode cell at 1980 MHz, but from M = 512 up the dense cells push it into the
700 W limit. A cell window is one cell's worker, BF16 and FP8 timed back to
back, read off the step log, and it counts as capped if one of its busy samples
carries the SW power-cap flag. In the run behind these tables the flag appears
in 71 of the 206 dense cell windows (every cell of the four dense files, the
unpublished `gate` and cubic rows included): in none up to M = 256, in 8 of 14
at M = 512, 13 of 15 at M = 1024, 14 of 15 at M = 2048 and in every window at
M = 4096 and 8192, where the clock fell as low as 1395 MHz (Family A `down` at
M = 8192). Those dense rows are
power-bound numbers rather than clock numbers; this is what the card does in
serving, and it is what the policy publishes. They also depend on the card's
recent power history: the bench times BF16 first and FP8 right after it, the
timed segment lasts only 0.01–0.2 s, and identical repeats of a capped cell
spread widely (Family A `gate_up` BF16 at M = 2048 read anywhere from 277 to
311 µs in the repeats of the 2026-10-01 release report). Do not accept or reject a change on
those rows without a clock sample of both arms, and keep the compile path the
same in both arms: in capped cells a kernel loaded from a warm JIT disk cache
read slower than one compiled in the same process.

dtypes on sm_90: BF16 = unquantized reference (`torch.nn.functional.linear`);
BSFP8 = block-scaled FP8, 1×128 activation / 128×128 weight scales, FP32
K-major scale layout, vendored deep_gemm JIT kernels (dense and grouped).
`µs` is the CUDA-graph replay median (50 replays × 3 repeats); `cos` is cosine
similarity vs the BF16 result on the same inputs; TFLOPS = `2·M·N·K / µs`.

#### `shared.down` (N=2048, K=512)

K = 512 is only four 128-wide scale blocks, so the per-block scale handling is
a fixed cost with little K to amortise it over, and storing the output is a
large part of the kernel's time. In this table BSFP8 is faster than BF16 at
every M, by 1.28–1.49× (2.59 against 3.68 µs at M = 1, 4.77 against 6.10 at
M = 1024, 22.09 against 32.88 at M = 8192). Before the sm_90 kernel round

## History

These notes describe tables and runs before the final 0.2.0 wheel; numbers
quoted in them are not the current tables.

**The sm_90 kernel round, 2026-10-05 (history).** The tables of this file were
re-measured from the final 0.2.0 wheel (commit `5253cd4`) in the run
`final-0.2.0-h200`, 18:05–18:18 UTC. The tables they replaced had been
installed at commit `6bc45a4` from the run `release-0.2.0-wheel-h200`,
05:19–05:34 UTC the same day, which measured the first 0.2.0 wheel (built at
`37e316b`). Both runs used GPU 0 of the local eight-H200 box (PCI `8F:00.0`),
the apex-0520 environment and the bundled NVRTC 13.2.78. Between the two wheels
two commits changed the dense sm_90 path (`12fc87d`, `24b2a1f`) and one the MoE
layer (`1eb550d`; its tables and its A/B are in `../layer/sm90.md`, History).
The dense outputs and the MoE layer outputs are bitwise identical to before.
The dense changes are these:
- **Output store.** The GEMM stages its output tile in shared memory as
  64-column slabs with the 128-byte swizzle, stores each slab with its own TMA
  store, and waits for that store only before the next tile reuses the buffer.
  Before, one thread stored the whole tile from an unswizzled buffer whose rows
  fell into the same shared-memory banks, and the CTA waited for the store
  before it started its next tile. This is the output path of DeepGEMM's
  `sm90_fp8_gemm_1d2d`.
- **Tile rules.** A 128-row tile that the picker chose 104–120 columns wide
  becomes 128 wide when that adds no wave. At M = 65–128 a 128-row tile 32
  columns wide or narrower becomes a 64-row tile twice as wide. On the swap-AB
  path of the smallest M the weight tile drops to 64 rows when 128-row tiles
  would give work to at most a quarter of the SMs (N ≤ 4224 on 132 SMs).
- **Activation quantize.** The 1×128 quantize gives each 128-element group to
  eight lanes; before, one thread walked several groups one after the other.
- **PDL.** The quantize and the GEMM are chained with programmatic dependent
  launch: the GEMM is launched while the quantize runs and waits for its input
  after its prologue. `FSO_DISABLE_PDL=1` turns it off.

The clean measure of these changes is an interleaved process-level A/B against
the first 0.2.0 wheel on the same card
(`/data/bench-runs/sm90_dg2_dense_20261005/PHASE2.md`, runs under
`phase2/runs/`). Over the 164 distinct dense cells (the eleven distinct shapes of
Families A, B and C at the 14 M of the grid, plus the cubic sweep) the new build
took 0.880 of the wheel's time at decode and 0.920 at prefill, as geometric
means. With PDL off, the kernel and tile changes alone took 0.955 and 0.929.
PDL on against off read 0.921 at decode and 0.990 at prefill on these cells,
and at decode that is the overlap of consecutive GEMMs in the rotation graph
described under Environment. The Family A MLP block, which chains the kernels
as a model does, took 0.826 of the wheel's time at decode and 0.911 at prefill.
The 64-row tiles at M = 65–128 were measured on top of that (section 7 of the
report) and made five cells 3.5–5.4 % faster.

Against the previous install the published BSFP8 cells moved by a geomean of
−14.3 % at decode and −9.8 % at prefill; all 168 of them moved by more than
1 %, and none got slower. The largest moves are on `shared.down` (−24.6 % in
geomean, −33.7 % at M = 4096) and on the narrow outputs at M ≤ 32, where the
64-row weight tile applies (−18.0 % to −26.2 %). Over the 164 cells of the A/B,
which include the unpublished `gate` and cubic rows, the baseline files moved by
0.874 at decode and 0.899 at prefill. The BF16 columns,
whose code did not change, moved by +0.1 % and +0.4 % in geomean, and single
prefill cells by −6.9 % to +10.2 % under the power cap. The two installs are
separate runs on the same card, so the table move mixes the code change with
this run-to-run spread; the A/B is the measure of the code change.
