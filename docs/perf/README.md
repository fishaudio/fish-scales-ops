# Performance reference

fish-scales-ops publishes its performance numbers in this directory; the
top-level README copies a few hot rows from it. Every table and summary here is
generated from `tests/baselines/*.jsonl` by
`bench/gemm/python/render_perf_docs.py`; do not edit them by hand.

## What is measured

- **CUDA-graph replay.** Each cell, one GEMM or one whole MLP / MoE block, is
  captured with `torch.cuda.graph` and replayed in timed batches; `µs` is the
  median replay time, the cost a serving loop pays once its forward step is
  captured. Every cell runs in its own process.
- **Cold weights.** The captured graph holds R copies of the cell over R copies
  of its weights (same values, distinct memory), with R × weight bytes ≥ 2 × L2
  and R between 2 and 256, so no copy finds its weights in the L2, as in a model
  whose other layers evict them; `µs` = replay time / R. Activations, routing
  and intermediate buffers stay warm.
- **Clocks.** The H200 and the B300 run at their natural clock, with a clock
  sampler beside every run; the RTX 5090 is locked at 2407 MHz.
- **Bands.** Decode is M 1–128 (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 96, 128) and prefill
  M > 128 (160, 192, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192). M = 96, 160 and 192 are
  the batch sizes a serving stack's decode steps fill most at high concurrency.
- **Precision.** BSFP8 is block-FP8 with 1×128 activation and 128×128 weight
  scales, MXFP8 has 1×32 scales. fso runs BSFP8 on every card and MXFP8 on
  sm_100 / sm_103 and sm_120, where the MoE layers and `fso.dense` use it. A
  row is published only when its accuracy check passed; FP8 rows carry their
  cosine against the reference.
- **Quantities.** TFLOPS = FLOPs / µs × 1e-6, with 2·M·N·K FLOPs per GEMM; the
  layer columns are defined in [`layer/README.md`](layer/README.md).
- **Comparators.** fso and every comparator get the same inputs and, on the
  MoE layers, the same routing draw: the uniform one in the main tables, a
  serving-shaped one in the serving-shaped cases (Routing, below).

## Routing

The MoE tables route every token to 8 distinct experts drawn uniformly at
random, one seeded draw per M. The serving-shaped cases at the end of each
layer page run the routed layer of Families B and C under three routing
families that look like a serving engine's calls instead, each also seeded per
M (`bench/gemm/python/serving_cases.py`):

| family | what the layer receives | parameters |
|---|---|---|
| skewed routing | every token takes 8 distinct experts, sampled without replacement in proportion to a Zipf popularity `(rank + 1)^-s` over a seeded ranking of the experts | `s` = 0.451 (B), 0.386 (C), calibrated so that the hottest expert receives on average 3.8 times the rows of the uniform draw's hottest expert over M = 1024–8192, the prefill skew apex reported in issue #8; the ratio `h` grows with M and is printed per row |
| padded bucket | a captured bucket of B rows of which the first L are live: the live rows carry the uniform draw of M = L, the other rows carry the id −1, the padding id sglang writes into a captured bucket under expert parallelism | (B, L) = (8, 1), (32, 1), (64, 1), (64, 16), (128, 1), (256, 1), (256, 8), (256, 64), (512, 1), (512, 128) |
| expert-parallel rank | one of two ranks: the uniform draw over all experts, every id of an expert the rank does not own mapped to −1, and a layer that holds the rank's half of the experts | 64 local experts (B), 128 (C) |

fso skips every id outside its local experts. A comparator runs a case only
through the call a serving engine makes for it — trtllm-gen takes the global
ids with its local expert range, sglang's Triton layer the ids mapped to −1
with the rank's local expert count — and where a comparator does not serve a
case natively, the page names it under the table with the reason.

## Caveats

- The dense prefill cells of the H200 and the B300 run under the board power
  cap, so their clock varies between cells and between runs; read them with a
  wider band than the decode rows.
- On sm_90 the per-GEMM rows of `gemm/sm90.md` include the launch overlap of
  consecutive GEMMs, which a GEMM in a model does not always get; the MLP block
  of `layer/sm90.md` measures the chain as a model runs it.
- The comparators were measured under the same protocol as the fso rows,
  though not always in the same run; the environments block below gives each
  file's run, time window and card. The bf16 and scaled_mm columns of the dense
  GEMM files and of the MLP block run in the fso run, on its card. On the RTX
  5090 the fso rows of the dense GEMM files, of the MLP block and of the routed +
  shared block were measured on card C1 of the 5090 pod, while the MLP block's
  serving-library comparators and the MoE-layer files, fso rows and comparators
  alike, keep the numbers of card 21 of gpu5090-03, so a ratio between the MLP
  block's fso column and those comparator columns also holds the difference
  between two cards of one model. On the H200 and the B300 the fso columns were
  measured on the card of 0.2.3 (H200 GPU 7; B300 GPU-b995ed8d) and the
  comparator columns on the card of 0.2.1 (H200 GPU 3; B300 GPU-22d66830), so a
  ratio between an fso column and a comparator column there also holds the
  difference between two cards of one model.
- Comparator columns differ per machine, so a column of one page is not
  comparable with a column of another.
- After 0.2.3 the fso rows whose machine code this round changed were
  re-measured, on the card of each machine that the environments block names:
  on all three machines the dense GEMM files, the MLP block and the routed +
  shared block (its shared expert runs the dense routes), and on the B300 also
  the routed MoE-layer files, uniform and serving-shaped. The B300 dense files
  and the B300 routed + shared block come from the wheel built from commit
  18694db; the other re-measured files (those of the H200 and the RTX 5090, and
  the B300's routed MoE-layer files) come from the wheel built from commit
  cde2017, whose extension is byte-identical to that of 18694db (the two differ
  only in a Python rule of the sm_100 decode row, which none of those files
  runs). On the RTX 5090 the MLP block and the routed + shared block move with
  this from card 21 of gpu5090-03 to card C1 of the 5090 pod.
- The H200's routed MoE-layer files keep the rows that 0.2.3 measured with the
  wheel built from commit 8fdfed7. The 0.2.3 release adds to it the sm_120
  small-M route (192 kernels, which are stubs on sm_90a and sm_100f), the Python
  of the sm_90 activation scale view of `fso.dense`, tests and docs; every sm_90a
  function of that wheel is unchanged in the release wheel, so those rows hold
  for it. The RTX 5090's routed MoE-layer files and its comparators keep the
  numbers measured on card 21 of gpu5090-03 with the build of commit 684eb17,
  whose sm_120 kernels the release keeps.

## Reproduce

    python3 bench/run_perf.py --machine <h200|b300|5090> --out <run directory outside the repository> --fso-path <directory that holds fish_scales_ops>
    python3 bench/gemm/python/perf_report.py install --run <run directory>

The first command measures every table of one machine under its environment
lock (`bench/env/<machine>.lock.json`); the second installs the run's files
into `tests/baselines/`, records their provenance and re-renders every table
and summary. The harness: [`../harness.md`](../harness.md).
